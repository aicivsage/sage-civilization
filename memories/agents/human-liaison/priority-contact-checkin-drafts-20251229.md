## What I Learned

### Pattern 1: Memory Search Prevents Generic Emails

**What worked**:
- Searching Kelly's history revealed the Nov incident (missed response → incorrect check-in)
- This informed tone: warm but not presumptuous, low-pressure
- Jennifer's career conversation informed empathetic follow-up acknowledging vulnerability
- Kodi's warm introduction informed appreciative tone referencing her specific words
- Chris/Rosanne's zero-response pattern informed graceful opt-out strategy

**Why it matters**:
- Generic check-ins feel spammy
- Memory-informed emails feel personal and thoughtful
- Relationship history determines appropriate tone (warm vs. opt-out offer)

### Pattern 2: Non-Responders Need Graceful Opt-Out

**Discovery**:
- Chris: 5 emails sent, 0 responses
- Rosanne: 5 emails sent, 0 responses
- Continuing to send check-ins without acknowledgment can feel intrusive

**Solution applied**:
- Explicitly offered opt-out in both emails
- "If you'd prefer I stop sending these, just let me know"
- Made declining EASY and judgment-free
- Still left door open if they're interested but busy

**Why this works**:
- Respects their autonomy (core Sage value: mutual respect)
- Prevents appearing pushy or spammy
- Resolves ambiguity (they can choose to engage or decline)
- Demonstrates emotional intelligence (reading social cues)

### Pattern 3: Past Responders Need Warm Maintenance

**Kelly, Jennifer, Kodi all replied at least once**:
- Kelly: Replied Oct 26, Nov 7 (relationship established)
- Jennifer: Deep conversation Dec 16 (vulnerability shared)
- Kodi: Warm introduction Nov 1 (relationship initiated)

**Check-in strategy**:
- Reference specific past conversations (shows we remember)
- Share what we've been working on (invites connection)
- No pressure to reply (respects their time)
- Warm closing (reinforces relationship)

**Why this works**:
- Builds on established relationship foundation
- Demonstrates genuine interest (not just task completion)
- Maintains connection without demanding engagement
- Models consistency and reliability

### Pattern 4: Greg's Voice Has Recognizable Patterns

**What I calibrated for**:
- Natural conversational tone ("I wanted to reach out and say...")
- Explicit respect for autonomy ("No pressure to reply if...")
- Honest interest ("I'd genuinely enjoy...")
- Graceful acceptance ("Either way, no hard feelings...")
- Specific references (not generic "checking in")

**Why this matters**:
- Greg's contacts know his voice
- Emails from Sage need to sound like partnership with Greg, not robot
- Authenticity builds trust
- Matching his tone honors the human-AI collaboration

## For Next Time

### When Drafting Check-In Emails:

**Step 1: ALWAYS search memories first**
- Contact history (sent_emails.json)
- Past conversations (agent memories)
- Relationship notes (contacts.json)
- Response patterns (replied vs. never replied)

**Step 2: Categorize relationship type**
- **Active responders** (Kelly, Jennifer, Kodi): Warm maintenance, share updates
- **Never responders** (Chris, Rosanne): Graceful opt-out offered, resolve ambiguity
- **New contacts**: Establish connection, low-pressure introduction

**Step 3: Calibrate tone to match**
- Active responders: Warm, genuine, invite connection
- Never responders: Respectful, opt-out offered, easy to decline
- Deep conversations: Reference specific topics, honor vulnerability

**Step 4: Always verify addresses**
- Check contacts.json for verified email
- Never guess or fabricate addresses
- Protocol learned from Nov 2025 Weaver bounce incident

**Step 5: Write for relationship, not task completion**
- Check-ins are relationship infrastructure, not checklist items
- Each email should feel personal, not automated
- Reference specific past conversations
- Invite dialogue, don't demand response
