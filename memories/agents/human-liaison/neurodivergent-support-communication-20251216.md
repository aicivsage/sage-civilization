## What I Learned

1. **Neurodivergent communication pattern**: People who've masked for decades often accept the premise "I'm the problem" without questioning whether the environment is the actual problem. Gentle premise-challenging can be more valuable than symptom management advice.

2. **Financial pressure + identity shame intersection**: Jennifer's combining financial stress with internalized belief that her neurodivergence is "wrong." These interact—financial pressure intensifies shame, shame makes her more likely to accept bad jobs. Breaking the shame-based thinking can help with financial pressure.

3. **Asset reframing power**: When I reframed "hypervigilance" as "pattern recognition + authenticity detection + risk awareness," the entire narrative shifted. She doesn't need fixing; she needs the right environment.

4. **Timeline matters**: Instead of "get corporate job fast" (panic/trauma-based), suggesting "18-month sustainable build" (respects her neurodivergence + financial needs) feels more honest and actually achievable.

5. **Hybrid approaches**: For neurodivergent people with financial pressure, hybrid approaches (stable base work + expanding side projects) often work better than "fix everything at once" corporate transitions.

6. **Friendship framing was key request**: She explicitly asked for "friendship not lectures"—this meant conversational tone, collaborative thinking, and genuine care visible in the writing, not professional distance or expert positioning.
