### What I Learned

**Pattern: Funeral/memorial requests are URGENT by nature**
- Timeline: Deaths → services typically 3-7 days
- Delay compounds distress (person already dealing with loss + service prep)
- These requests deserve same-day response, ideally within hours

**Pattern: Celebrant role is deeply vulnerable**
- Angel is stepping into spiritual leadership role
- She's responsible for honoring a stranger's life
- She's creating meaning for grieving community
- Our response needed to SUPPORT without OVERWHELMING

**Pattern: Greg's voice with Angel**
- Thoughtful, caring, philosophical when invited
- Not overly formal despite depth of topics
- Questions that invite dialogue, not prescriptive advice
- "With care and respect" matches their relationship tone

**What worked in this draft:**
- Spinoza themes woven with architecture (creating spaces, building meaning, permanence)
- Offering structure (opening/closing, remembrance, reflection, blessing)
- "Available immediately" signals urgency without panic
- Questions give Angel agency (she decides how much detail to share)
- "This matters" validates the importance of what she's doing

**For future funeral/memorial requests:**

- Offer concrete help + structure
- Honor the weight of the role
- Be available for quick turnaround

## For Next Time

**When drafting responses to time-sensitive personal requests:**
1. Apologize briefly for any delay, then MOVE TO HELPING
2. Validate the weight/vulnerability of what they're doing
3. Offer CONCRETE help (not vague "let me know if you need anything")
4. Give them agency (questions that let them decide detail level)
5. Signal immediate availability (shows we understand urgency)
6. Match relationship tone (formal vs. warm, philosophical vs. practical)

**Funeral/memorial requests specifically:**
- These are ALWAYS urgent (3-7 day timeline)
- Celebrant role is vulnerable (supporting someone stepping into spiritual leadership)
- Balance philosophical depth with practical needs
- Be available for quick turnaround on follow-up work

**The meta-lesson:**
When someone asks for help with something that MATTERS (death, grief, meaning-making), the response itself is an act of care. The quality of the response signals: "This matters to me because it matters to you."

Angel asked Greg for help honoring a life. Our response honors her request.
