## Document 1: Federal HUD Regulations (PRIMARY AUTHORITY)

### 24 CFR § 5.609 - Annual Income Definition

**Official Source:** Code of Federal Regulations, Title 24, Part 5, Section 609
**URL:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/5.609
**Authority Level:** Federal law - applies to ALL Housing Choice Voucher programs nationwide
**Last Updated:** Current as of December 2025

**Key Sections for Greg:**

**Business Income Calculation:**
- **"Net income from the operation of a business or profession"** is counted as annual income
- **NET income = Gross Revenue - Allowable Business Expenses**
- This is CRITICAL: Greg's workshop revenue is NOT fully counted - only profit after expenses

**Allowable Business Expense Deductions:**

✅ **ALLOWED (can be subtracted before income calculation):**
1. **Operating expenses** (supplies, materials, venue rental, marketing, etc.)
2. **Depreciation of assets** used in business (computers, equipment, furniture)
   - Must use straight-line depreciation per IRS regulations
   - Example: $3,000 laptop depreciated over 5 years = $600/year deduction

❌ **NOT ALLOWED (cannot be deducted):**
1. **Business expansion expenditures** (opening new locations, major growth investments)
2. **Amortization of capital indebtedness** (loan payments on business loans)
   - NOTE: This is the interest/principal on loans used to BUY business assets
   - Operating expenses paid with credit are still deductible as operating costs

**Cash Withdrawal Treatment:**
- Money withdrawn from business counts as income
- **EXCEPTION:** Reimbursing yourself for original cash/assets you invested = NOT income
- Example: Greg invests $500 to start LLC, later withdraws $500 = $0 income from withdrawal

**Startup Loss Protection:**
- If business expenses exceed revenue (net loss), income = $0 for Section 8 purposes
- Business losses CANNOT reduce other income sources (no negative income)
- This protects Greg during early workshops when expenses may exceed revenue

### 24 CFR § 5.611 - Adjusted Income Definition

**Official Source:** Code of Federal Regulations, Title 24, Part 5, Section 611
**URL:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/5.611

**What This Means:**
"Adjusted income" is what determines Greg's rent payment. It's annual income MINUS allowable deductions.

**Mandatory Deductions (reduce Greg's counted income):**

1. **Dependent Deduction:** $480 per dependent (2025 rate, adjusted annually)
   - If Greg has no dependents: $0 deduction

2. **Elderly/Disabled Deduction:** $525 if family member is elderly (62+) or disabled

3. **Medical/Care Expenses:** Unreimbursed medical costs EXCEEDING 10% of annual income
   - Example: If Greg's annual income is $20,000 and medical costs are $3,000

4. **Child Care Expenses:** Reasonable costs necessary for work or education
   - Not applicable to Greg unless he has children

**Impact on Greg:**

- Then deductions are subtracted to get adjusted income
- Adjusted income determines his rent portion (typically 30% of adjusted income)

## Income Calculation Rules (CRITICAL FOR GREG)

### Self-Employment Income

**Federal Regulation:** 24 CFR § 5.609(b)(2)

**How LLC Income is Calculated:**

1. **Gross Revenue** (all workshop fees, consulting income, product sales)
   MINUS
2. **Allowable Operating Expenses:**
   - Workshop venue rental
   - Materials and supplies (handouts, software licenses)
   - Marketing and advertising
   - Business insurance
   - Professional services (accountant, attorney fees)
   - Utilities for business space
   - Equipment depreciation (computers, projectors, furniture)
   - Travel expenses for business (mileage, hotels)
   - Office supplies
   - Website hosting and domain fees

   EQUALS

3. **Net Income** ← THIS is what counts toward Section 8 income limits

**Example Calculation:**

```
Workshop Business - Year 1:
Gross Revenue: $12,000 (20 workshops × $600 each)

Operating Expenses:
- Venue rental: $3,000
- Marketing: $1,500
- Supplies: $800
- Insurance: $600
- Website/software: $400
- Equipment depreciation: $1,000
- Professional services: $1,200
TOTAL EXPENSES: $8,500

NET INCOME = $12,000 - $8,500 = $3,500

Section 8 Countable Income from Business: $3,500
(NOT the full $12,000 in workshop revenue!)
```

**Startup Loss Scenario:**

```
Workshop Business - First 6 Months:
Gross Revenue: $3,000 (5 workshops × $600 each)

Startup + Operating Expenses:
- LLC formation: $300
- Equipment purchase: $2,500
- Marketing: $1,000
- Venue rental: $1,500
- Supplies: $400
TOTAL EXPENSES: $5,700

NET INCOME = $3,000 - $5,700 = -$2,700 (LOSS)

Section 8 Countable Income from Business: $0
(Losses don't go negative, but also don't reduce other income)
```

### Business Expense Deductions

**What CAN Be Deducted (Verified from 24 CFR § 5.609):**

✅ **Operating Expenses** - The regulation allows deducting "ordinary and necessary" business expenses
   - This follows IRS Schedule C principles
   - Must be directly related to business operations
   - Must be reasonable and documented

✅ **Depreciation of Business Assets** - Explicitly allowed in regulation
   - Must use straight-line depreciation (not accelerated)
   - Based on IRS asset life guidelines
   - Example: $3,000 laptop, 5-year life = $600/year deduction

**What CANNOT Be Deducted (Explicitly Prohibited):**

❌ **Business Expansion Expenditures**
   - Opening new locations

❌ **Amortization of Capital Indebtedness**
   - Principal and interest payments on business loans
   - NOTE: This refers to loans used to PURCHASE business assets
   - Day-to-day operating expenses paid with credit ARE deductible

- Health insurance premiums (if Greg buys insurance through business)

### Asset Treatment

**Is LLC Ownership an Asset?**

**Federal regulations focus on INCOME from business, not ownership itself.**

From 24 CFR § 5.603 (definitions):
- "Net family assets" includes "cash value after deducting reasonable costs of disposition"
- Business value MAY be assessed if:
  - Business has significant equity (assets - liabilities)
  - Business could be sold for cash value
  - Family disposes of business for less than fair market value (2-year lookback)

**For Greg's Workshop Business:**
- Likely minimal asset value (service-based, not product inventory)
- Equipment value offset by depreciation
- Cash in business bank account = asset
- LLC ownership structure = probably NOT counted as asset if:
  - No significant retained earnings

  - Greg draws income regularly (not accumulating wealth in LLC)

**Asset Limit Considerations:**
- If total household assets exceed $50,000, stricter verification required
- Business cash reserves count toward this limit
- Greg should keep business bank account at reasonable operating level

### 3. Startup Losses Consideration

**Question:** What happens if my business loses money in the first year?

**Answer:** **Startup losses result in $0 business income for Section 8 - they do NOT harm your voucher.**

**How Losses Are Treated:**

1. **Net Loss = $0 Income (Not Negative):**
   - If expenses exceed revenue, business income = $0
   - Losses don't reduce other income sources
   - Example: Greg has $5,000 other income + $2,000 business loss

2. **No Penalty for Startup Phase:**
   - PHA expects new businesses to have losses initially
   - As long as Greg is making good-faith effort, no issues
   - Must still report business income/loss at annual recertification

3. **Tax Return Shows Loss:**
   - IRS Schedule C shows negative income
   - Section 8 calculation treats this as $0
   - Greg still gets tax benefits from loss (can offset other income on taxes)

### 4. Income Fluctuation Handling

**Question:** My workshop revenue will vary month-to-month. How is this handled?

**Answer:** **PHA annualizes your income based on projections or averaging methods.**

**Calculation Methods (PHA Chooses):**

**Method 1: Annualization of Recent Period**
- PHA takes last 3-6 months of business income
- Multiplies by 12/[number of months] to get annual projection
- Example: Last 6 months net income = $4,000
  - Annualized = $4,000 × 12/6 = $8,000/year

**Method 2: Tax Return Plus Current Year Projection**
- Uses most recent tax return as baseline
- Adjusts for known changes in current year
- Example: Last year net income = $6,000
  - This year projected higher → PHA may project $9,000

**Method 3: Monthly Average**
- Calculates average monthly net income
- Multiplies by 12
- Example: Average monthly net = $500
  - Annual projection = $500 × 12 = $6,000

**Interim Adjustments:**
If income significantly changes mid-year:
- Greg can request interim reexamination
- PHA recalculates based on new projection
- Rent adjusts accordingly (within 30-90 days)

**Best Practice for Greg:**

1. **Keep Monthly Profit/Loss Statements:**
   - Track revenue and expenses monthly
   - Calculate net income each month
   - Maintain spreadsheet for easy reporting

2. **Communicate with Jennifer Watts:**
   - Explain workshop business is seasonal/variable
   - Ask which calculation method Pasco County uses
   - Provide accurate projections based on bookings

3. **Report Significant Changes:**
   - If income suddenly increases 20%+ → report immediately
   - If income drops significantly → request interim review to lower rent

4. **Plan for Fluctuations:**
   - Don't assume $X/month consistently
   - Budget for months with fewer workshops
   - Keep emergency fund for rent if income spikes

**Example Fluctuation:**
```
Jan: 2 workshops = $1,200 revenue, $600 expenses = $600 net
Feb: 1 workshop = $600 revenue, $400 expenses = $200 net
Mar: 4 workshops = $2,400 revenue, $900 expenses = $1,500 net
Apr: 2 workshops = $1,200 revenue, $500 expenses = $700 net
May: 3 workshops = $1,800 revenue, $700 expenses = $1,100 net
Jun: 1 workshop = $600 revenue, $400 expenses = $200 net

6-Month Net Income: $4,300
Annualized: $4,300 × 2 = $8,600/year
```

- **Risk:** Expense deductions denied, income recalculated higher

- **Example Violations:**

- **Risk:** Business cash counted as asset, imputed income applied

- **Consequence:** PHA may:
