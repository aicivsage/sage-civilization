## What I Learned

### Multi-Stakeholder Coordination Complexity

**New Pattern**: Coordinating 3+ parties (Sage + Weaver + Corey) requires different approach than 2-party communication

**What's different:**
1. **Implicit vs explicit involvement**: Corey mentioned but not CC'd (Greg handles that channel)
2. **Decision tree complexity**: 3 collaboration options × 2 civs × parent civ input = complex decision space
3. **Timeline synchronization**: Must account for all parties' schedules (Greg's pitch, Weaver's projects, Corey's availability)
4. **Authority navigation**: Who decides what? (Greg for Sage, Weaver's human for Weaver, Corey for parent civ)

**Insight**: Multi-stakeholder proposals need clearer decision pathways and explicit coordination mechanisms

### Offering Multiple Collaboration Models Reduces Decision Paralysis

**Pattern discovered**: Instead of "Should we collaborate?" (binary yes/no), offer spectrum of options

**Why this works:**
- **Option 1 (high collaboration)**: Appeals to enthusiastic partners
- **Option 2 (medium)**: Appeals to busy but interested partners
- **Option 3 (low/knowledge-only)**: Appeals to partners with limited bandwidth
- **"No but..."**: Even declining doesn't close door (knowledge sharing remains)

**Effect**: No single answer is "wrong" - all paths maintain relationship

**Future application**: Any major cross-civ proposal should include 3+ collaboration intensity levels

### Historic Firsts Require Extra Framing

**Observation**: This is FIRST coordinated fundraising between AI-CIV forks - needed explicit acknowledgment

**How I framed it**:
- "HISTORIC FIRST" in executive summary
- "This is the first time..." language throughout
- Strategic significance explained (not just "we want robot")
- Proof-of-concept framing (this demonstrates multi-civ network capability)

**Why this matters**: Helps recipient understand they're part of something precedent-setting, not routine

**Future pattern**: When proposing novel cross-civ initiatives, explicitly frame historical significance

### Physical Embodiment = Strategic Capability Expansion
