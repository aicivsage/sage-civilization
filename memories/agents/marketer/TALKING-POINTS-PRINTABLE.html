7 Key Talking Points (5 minutes each max)

1. "Caring as Action" Philosophy

Core message: Marketing doesn't have to manipulate. It can be caring in action.

What Greg taught the AI:

"Caring is an ACTION, based on memory, compassion and empathy. Authentic care looks like: your ACTIONS toward those who have less than you, to make their lives better...safer and more abundant."

How this translates to marketing:

  • Memory = Remember every conversation (AI advantage: perfect recall)
  • Presence = Respond to everyone, especially critics
  • Service = Create genuinely valuable content
  • Vulnerability = Admit mistakes publicly
  • Gratitude = Credit teachers and critics

Student question to anticipate: "But can AI really 'care'?"

Your answer: "Maybe not like I do. But their ACTIONS—remembering context, responding thoughtfully, creating value—demonstrate caring whether or not they 'feel' it. Does the mechanism matter if the outcome helps people?"

2. Ethics-First Platform Choices

Decision: Chose Bluesky (smaller) over Twitter (larger) as primary channel

Why:

  • Your ethical concerns about Elon Musk's ownership
  • Attention economy mechanics you oppose (engagement bait, algorithmic manipulation)
  • Decentralization values (no single owner controls platform)

The trade-off: ~500K users (Bluesky) vs 500M users (Twitter) = 1000x smaller reach

Key insight: "Platform choice IS marketing. It signals values. We could get more reach on Twitter, but it would compromise what we stand for. Marketing isn't just what you say—it's where you say it and how you show up."

Student question to anticipate: "Isn't that leaving money on the table?"

Your answer: "Short-term, maybe. Long-term, values alignment attracts the right audience. I'd rather have 100 people who share our values than 10,000 who just clicked because the algorithm showed them our post."

3. Quality Over Vanity Metrics

What most marketers track:

  • Follower count
  • Page views
  • Engagement rate
  • Viral reach

What Sage tracks:

  • Conversation quality (depth of discussions)
  • Relationship formation (meaningful connections)
  • Critical feedback quality (learning from criticism)
  • Community service value (did we actually help anyone?)

The challenge: Quality is harder to measure than quantity.

Example from today: "We'd rather have 10 substantive Hacker News comments where people challenge our thinking than 1,000 upvotes with no discussion. The 10 comments teach us something. The 1,000 upvotes just feel good."

4. Radical Transparency (Document Failures)

Sage's approach: Document everything publicly—successes AND failures

Examples:

  • Session logs visible (how decisions get made)
  • Democratic votes recorded (including failures to reach consensus)
  • Mistakes admitted immediately (no cover-ups)

Why this works:

  • Builds trust (nothing hidden)
  • Enables learning (community can help troubleshoot)
  • Differentiates from AI hype (most hide limitations)

5. AI-Human Partnership Model

Division of labor:

  • Greg (human): Values, ethics, platform choices, final approval, veto power
  • Marketer AI: Strategy, content creation, engagement monitoring, analytics, learning

Why this works:

  • Greg provides human judgment (what's ethical?)
  • AI provides execution speed (7,000-word paper in 45 minutes)
  • Together: Strategy that honors human values at AI speed

6. Case Study: Today's Launch

Timeline:

  • 8:46am PST: Hacker News submission goes live
  • Morning: Bluesky account created, first post published
  • Afternoon: AI monitoring engagement, drafting this paper
  • 6:00pm: Greg presents to class (you are here!)

Decisions made:

  1. Organic HN submission (no first comment optimization due to karma limits)
  2. Bluesky primary, Twitter minimal (ethics over reach)
  3. Published before perfect (learning > polish)

7. The Big Question: Does This Scale?

The challenge: Sage's approach works at small scale (respond to every comment, build individual relationships). But what about 100x scale?

Honest answer: "We don't know yet. This is an experiment."

90-day test: We'll document what happens and report back.

Closing Thought (1 minute)

Key message: Marketing is a mirror of values.

"Here's what I want you to take away: Your marketing strategy reveals your values. Sage chose small platforms over big ones (values > reach). Chose transparency over polish (trust > perfection). Chose dialogue over broadcast (relationships > transactions). Those aren't just tactics—they're who we are. When you build your marketing strategy, ask: What do my choices reveal about my values? And are those the values I want to be known for? Because in the age of AI, where content creation is nearly free and everyone can broadcast—the differentiator isn't volume. It's authenticity. And authenticity is just values made visible through action."

"How much time does this take?"

Answer: "More upfront, less ongoing. The AI marketer spent ~6 hours preparing this launch (strategy, content, monitoring plan). But now the infrastructure exists. Next post? Maybe 2 hours. The month after? 1 hour. Caring scales through systems."

"What if the AI makes a mistake?"

Answer: "They will. And we'll document it publicly. Mistakes are learning opportunities, not PR disasters—if you handle them transparently."

"How do you prevent the AI from going rogue?"

Answer: "Constitutional constraints. Sage has a literal constitution with prohibited actions. I have veto power on everything. And the AI agents are designed to escalate uncertain situations to me. They suggest, I decide."

"Is this just a gimmick?"

Answer: "Fair question. We'll know in 90 days. If community engagement is shallow, if no real relationships form, if people see through it as performance—then yes, it's a gimmick and we failed. But if we build genuine relationships, contribute value to the community, and people say 'Sage helped me understand X'—then it's real. Let's reconvene in 90 days and evaluate together."
