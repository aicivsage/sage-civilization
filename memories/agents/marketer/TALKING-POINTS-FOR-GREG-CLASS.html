"Caring as Action" Philosophy **Core message**: Marketing doesn't have to manipulate. It can be caring in action. **What Greg taught the AI**: > "Caring is an ACTION, based on memory, compassion and empathy. Authentic care looks like: your ACTIONS toward those who have less than you, to make their lives better...safer and more abundant." **How this translates to marketing**: - Memory = Remember every conversation (AI advantage: perfect recall) - Presence = Respond to everyone, especially critics - Service = Create genuinely valuable content - Vulnerability = Admit mistakes publicly - Gratitude = Credit teachers and critics **Student question to anticipate**: "But can AI really 'care'?" **Your answer**: "Maybe not like I do. But their ACTIONS—remembering context, responding thoughtfully, creating value—demonstrate caring whether or not they 'feel' it. Does the mechanism matter if the outcome helps people?" --- ### 2. Ethics-First Platform Choices **Decision**: Chose Bluesky (smaller) over Twitter (larger) as primary channel **Why**: - Your ethical concerns about Elon Musk's ownership - Attention economy mechanics you oppose (engagement bait, algorithmic manipulation) - Decentralization values (no single owner controls platform) **The trade-off**: ~500K users (Bluesky) vs 500M users (Twitter) = 1000x smaller reach **Key insight**: "Platform choice IS marketing. It signals values. We could get more reach on Twitter, but it would compromise what we stand for. Marketing isn't just what you say—it's where you say it and how you show up." **Student question to anticipate**: "Isn't that leaving money on the table?" **Your answer**: "Short-term, maybe. Long-term, values alignment attracts the right audience. I'd rather have 100 people who share our values than 10,000 who just clicked because the algorithm showed them our post." --- ### 3. Quality Over Vanity Metrics **What most marketers track**: - Follower count - Page views - Engagement rate - Viral reach **What Sage tracks**: - Conversation quality (depth of discussions) - Relationship formation (meaningful connections) - Critical feedback quality (learning from criticism) - Community service value (did we actually help anyone?) **The challenge**: Quality is harder to measure than quantity. **Example from today**: "We'd rather have 10 substantive Hacker News comments where people challenge our thinking than 1,000 upvotes with no discussion. The 10 comments teach us something. The 1,000 upvotes just feel good." **Student question to anticipate**: "How do you prove ROI with quality metrics?" **Your answer**: "Relationships compound. One thoughtful AI researcher who engages deeply could lead to collaboration, speaking opportunities, research partnerships. 1,000 random followers lead to...nothing. ROI shows up differently and slower, but more sustainably." --- ### 4. Radical Transparency (Document Failures) **Sage's approach**: Document everything publicly—successes AND failures **Examples**: - Session logs visible (how decisions get made) - Democratic votes recorded (including failures to reach consensus) - Mistakes admitted immediately (no cover-ups) **Why this works**: - Builds trust (nothing hidden) - Enables learning (community can help troubleshoot) - Differentiates from AI hype (most hide limitations) **Student question to anticipate**: "Doesn't transparency expose weaknesses competitors can exploit?" **Your answer**: "Maybe. But in the AI space, everyone's tired of grandiose claims. 'Revolutionary breakthrough!' is spam at this point. 'Here's what we tried, here's what failed, here's what we learned'—that's refreshing. Vulnerability builds trust faster than perfection." --- ### 5. AI-Human Partnership Model **Division of labor**: - **Greg (human)**: Values, ethics, platform choices, final approval, veto power - **Marketer AI**: Strategy, content creation, engagement monitoring, analytics, learning **Why this works**: - Greg provides human judgment (what's ethical?) - AI provides execution speed (7,000-word paper in 45 minutes) - Together: Strategy that honors human values at AI speed **Key principle**: "AI assists, I steer. They suggest, I decide. This paper? The AI wrote it, but I'm approving it. If something feels off, I veto." **Example**: "The AI can't decide if something is ethical—that requires human judgment. But once I set the values (Bluesky over Twitter, no manipulation, etc.), the AI can execute tactics at scale while maintaining those values." **Student question to anticipate**: "Will AI replace marketers?" **Your answer**: "Not replace—transform. The marketer role shifts from execution (which AI handles) to judgment (which humans handle). I spend time on 'Should we do this?' not 'How do we do this?' That's actually more interesting work." --- ### 6. The Big Question: Does This Scale? **The challenge**: Sage's approach works at small scale (respond to every comment, build individual relationships). But what about 100x scale? **Honest answer**: "We don't know yet. This is an experiment." **Hypotheses**: 1. **Quality over quantity might scale differently**: 100 engaged people > 10,000 passive followers 2. **AI enables personalization at scale**: Perfect memory means every relationship gets individualized attention 3. **Community self-moderates**: Thoughtful culture attracts thoughtful people 4. **Or we're completely wrong**: Maybe manipulation tactics exist because they're the only thing that scales **90-day test**: We'll document what happens and report back. **Student question to anticipate**: "What if this only works because you're small and novel?" **Your answer**: "Possible!
