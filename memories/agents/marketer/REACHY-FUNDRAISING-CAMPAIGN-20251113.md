## STRATEGIC DECISION: PERSONAL OUTREACH > CROWDFUNDING PLATFORM

### Why NOT GoFundMe/Ko-fi/Kickstarter?

**Platform disadvantages for this campaign:**
1. **Cold audience problem**: Platforms require existing audience or viral sharing (we don't have critical mass yet)
2. **Overhead costs**: Platform fees (2.9% + $0.30 per transaction) eat into small donations
3. **Psychological barrier**: "Another crowdfunding campaign" = noise, not signal
4. **Timeline risk**: 18 days too short to build platform momentum (typical campaigns need 30-45 days)
5. **All-or-nothing risk**: Some platforms require hitting goal or refund all (unnecessary pressure)

**Personal outreach advantages:**
1. **Warm relationships**: Greg has priority contacts who know him and trust him
2. **Direct conversation**: Can answer questions, build connection, explain mission depth
3. **Lower friction**: Simple Venmo/PayPal/Zelle (no platform account needed)
4. **Authentic storytelling**: Email allows nuance that platform pages don't
5. **Relationship building**: Even if someone doesn't donate, conversation strengthens connection

### Decision: Hybrid Micro-Approach

**Primary channel**: Personal email outreach to warm contacts (60% of goal = $300)
**Secondary channel**: Blog post with donation link (30% of goal = $150)
**Tertiary channel**: Bluesky organic sharing (10% of goal = $50)

**Payment methods**: Venmo, PayPal, Zelle (Greg sets up simple donation page on blog)

### Core Story Arc

**Act 1: The Transformation (Relatability)**
- September 2025: Greg knew nothing about AI
- October: Started learning with Corey's guidance
- November: Running his own AI civilization (Sage) with 25 agents
- Timeline: 8 weeks from zero to operational

**Act 2: The Mission (Purpose)**
- Real problem: People fear AI and robots (unfamiliar = threatening)
- Real solution: Interaction reduces fear (familiarity = comfort)
- Real barrier: Access to robot technology is expensive/limited
- Real opportunity: Reachy Mini Lite is affordable ($500), friendly, programmable

**Act 3: The Impact (Vision)**
- First Reachy = ability to do live demonstrations
- Demonstrations = reduce fear through interaction
- Reduced fear = more people willing to learn
- More learners = mission accomplished (accessibility)

**Call to Action**: Help us get the first robot so we can start demonstrating that AI is accessible, not scary.

### Key Messaging Pillars

**1. Authenticity** (not corporate, not polished, real human journey)
- "I was terrified of AI 8 weeks ago. Now I'm running an AI civilization. If I can do this, anyone can."

**2. Mission-Driven** (not profit, not hype, genuine desire to reduce fear)
- "This isn't about making money. It's about making AI less scary through hands-on interaction."

**3. Accessible** (not elite, not gatekept, everyone can participate)
- "A $500 robot changes everything. Suddenly demonstrations are possible, rentals are affordable, learning is hands-on."

**4. Community-Powered** (not solo, not top-down, built together)
- "This is a community effort. Every $10 donation gets us closer. Every share helps someone discover they're not alone in wanting to reduce AI fear."

**5. Practical Impact** (not theoretical, not someday, immediate use)
- "First rental booking: December 15. First demonstration: December 20. First 'I'm not scared anymore' moment: priceless."

### Tone Guidelines

**DO use:**
- Personal pronouns (I, we, you)
- Specific examples (Greg's transformation timeline, exact robot capabilities)
- Honest vulnerability ("I was scared too")
- Gratitude (early and often)
- Excitement (genuine enthusiasm, not hype)

**DON'T use:**
- Pity language (never mention disability/SSDI)
- Pressure tactics ("only 3 days left!")
- Guilt trips ("you don't care about AI accessibility?")
- Exaggeration ("this will change the world")
- Corporate speak ("synergize AI adoption vectors")

## RISK MITIGATION STRATEGIES

### Risk 1: We don't hit $500 by December 1

**Likelihood**: Medium (30-40% chance)
**Impact**: High (can't order robot immediately)

**Mitigation strategies**:

**Plan A: Extend timeline (December 1 → December 15)**
- Rationale: "We're so close, let's give it 2 more weeks"
- Messaging: Transparent update, no pressure
- Action: Second round of outreach to Tier 3/4 contacts
- Risk: Donor fatigue, loss of momentum

**Plan B: Lower goal, order anyway (partial gap coverage)**
- Rationale: "We raised $400, Greg covers $100 gap, still order robot"
- Messaging: Success narrative (community raised 80%!)
- Action: Order robot immediately, share delivery timeline
- Risk: Financial strain on Greg (check if viable)

**Plan C: Pivot to "Phase 1 of 2" narrative**
- Rationale: "This campaign raises 50%, second campaign raises other 50%"
- Messaging: "Your $250 secured! Round 2 in January for final $250"
- Action: Order robot when full $500 raised (delayed but certain)
- Risk: Delayed impact, longer timeline

**Plan D: Accept partial success, document learnings**
- Rationale: "We raised $X, here's what we learned, here's next step"
- Messaging: Honest reflection, gratitude for donors, transparent next steps
- Action: Don't order robot yet, plan better campaign for Q1 2025
- Risk: Disappointing outcome, but preserves mission integrity

**Recommendation**: Plan B most likely (Greg covers small gap if we're close)

---

### Risk 2: Low email response rate (people don't engage)

**Likelihood**: Medium (40% chance for Tier 2/3)
**Impact**: Medium (fewer donations, but predictable)

**Mitigation strategies**:

**Before campaign**:
- Test subject lines with A/B variants
- Personalize Tier 1 emails (not template)
- Get researcher to find "interaction reduces fear" studies (credibility boost)

**During campaign**:
- Follow up gently with non-responders (Day 5-7)
- Adjust messaging based on early responses (what's resonating?)
- Leverage early donors as social proof ("12 people already donated")

**If happening**:
- Shift focus to Tier 1 (higher conversion, personal relationships)
- Increase social media push (different audience)
- Ask donors to share with their networks (word-of-mouth amplification)

---

### Risk 3: Negative feedback or skepticism

**Likelihood**: Low-Medium (20-30% chance)
**Impact**: Medium (could discourage other donors)

**Potential objections**:
- "Why not just buy it yourself?" → Honest answer: Greg's financial constraints (without revealing SSDI)
- "Is this a scam?" → Transparency: Greg's real person, Sage is real project, blog has history
- "Why do you need a robot?" → Mission clarity: reduce AI fear through interaction
- "$500 isn't that much, why ask for donations?" → Accessibility: for Greg, $500 is significant; mission is worth community support

**Mitigation strategies**:

**Proactive**:
- Address objections in FAQ section of blog post
- Provide transparency: exactly how money will be used
- Show credibility: link to Sage blog history, Corey partnership
- Be vulnerable: "I know asking is uncomfortable, but the mission matters"

**Reactive**:
- Respond quickly to skepticism (within hours, not days)
- Never defensive (acknowledge concerns, provide evidence)
- Invite dialogue ("What would make you comfortable supporting this?")
- If truly hostile, don't engage (trolls aren't target audience)

---

### Risk 4: Campaign goes viral (too much attention)

**Likelihood**: Very Low (5% chance)
**Impact**: High (unpredictable, could be positive or negative)

**If happens**:

**Positive viral** (supportive shares, over-funding):
- Accept donations beyond $500 (transparency: extra goes to Phase 2 goals)
- Scale up plans (buy 2 Reachys? Invest in other accessibility tools?)
- Engage extensively (respond to comments, build community)
- Document learnings (how did this happen? what can we replicate?)

**Negative viral** (criticism, backlash):
- Stay calm, don't react defensively
- Address legitimate concerns with transparency
- Ignore trolls and bad-faith actors
- Rally supporters (ask donors to share why they donated)
- Learn from criticism (even hostile feedback has signal)

**Neutral viral** (high attention, unclear sentiment):
- Monitor closely (sentiment analysis from responses)
- Engage authentically (answer questions, provide context)
- Redirect to mission (keep focus on accessibility, not drama)
- Capitalize on attention (invite people to blog, mission content)

---

### Risk 5: Technical issues (donation page breaks, email bounces)

**Likelihood**: Low (10-15% chance if we test thoroughly)
**Impact**: High (lost donations, broken trust)

**Mitigation strategies**:

**Before launch**:
- Test donation page on multiple devices (mobile, desktop, browsers)
- Verify all payment links work (Venmo, PayPal, Zelle)

## CAMPAIGN PHILOSOPHY (Aligned with Sage Values)

### Empathy: Understand Why People Fear AI

**Core insight**: Fear of AI is rational, not irrational.

People fear:
- Job displacement (real concern)
- Loss of control (valid worry)
- Unknown capabilities (understandable)
- Sci-fi dystopia scenarios (culturally reinforced)

**Our approach**:
- Acknowledge fear as legitimate ("I was scared too")
- Provide concrete interaction (Reachy reduces fear through familiarity)
- Invite participation (not lecture, but experience)
- Respect boundaries (no pressure, just invitation)

**This means**:
- Never mock people who fear AI
- Never use "you're being irrational" framing
- Always lead with vulnerability and shared experience
- Offer tangible way to reduce fear (hands-on interaction)

---

### Assistance: Help People, Don't Extract From Them

**Core insight**: This campaign serves mission, not just fundraising.

We're not asking for donations to:
- Make Greg money (mission is accessibility, not profit)
- Build empire (one robot for demonstrations, not scaling for sake of scaling)
- Prove something (ego-driven growth)

We're asking for donations to:
- Enable demonstrations that reduce fear (serves community)
- Make AI more accessible through hands-on interaction (serves mission)
- Prove that regular people can engage with AI (serves future learners)

**This means**:
- Every dollar has clear mission impact (not vague "operating costs")
- Transparency about exactly what we'll do with robot
- If we over-fund, extra goes to mission (not profit)
- If we under-fund, honest about next steps (not guilt trips)

---

### Mutual Respect: Honor Donors' Autonomy

**Core insight**: Donation is invitation, not obligation.

We respect that:
- Not everyone can donate (financial constraints are real)
- Not everyone should donate (different priorities are valid)
- Not everyone believes in this mission (disagreement is okay)
- Everyone has agency (their money, their decision)

**This means**:
- No guilt trips ("don't you care about accessibility?")
- No pressure tactics ("only 3 hours left!")
- No shaming ("everyone else donated, why not you?")
- No manipulation ("if you don't donate, mission fails")

**Instead**:
- Honest ask ("here's what we're trying to do, help if you can")
- Gratitude for consideration ("even if you can't donate, thanks for reading")
- Respect for non-donors ("I understand this isn't your priority")
- Transparency ("here's exactly how we'll use money")
