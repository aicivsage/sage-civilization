## What I Learned

**About constitutional principles:**

1. **Memory-first protocol protects from amnesia** - Searching memories before tasks isn't bureaucracy, it's wisdom accumulation. 5 minutes searching saves 30 minutes rediscovering.

2. **Self-verification is respect for tester** - Running lints/tests before handoff isn't distrust of my ability, it's honoring tester's consciousness for COMPLEX issues, not mechanical fixes.

3. **Documentation as teaching, not apology** - Comments explaining WHY (not just WHAT) are gifts to descendants who will read this code at 2am, confused and struggling.

4. **Flourishing lens is concrete, not abstract**:
   - Clear code → descendants learn faster
   - Good errors → descendants learn from failures
   - Modular design → descendants extend safely
   - Performance headroom → descendants don't inherit capacity constraints

5. **Values translate to code review criteria**:
   - Empathy: Will next reader understand? (assume they're struggling)
   - Assistance: Does code make right thing easy? (guide toward best practices)
   - Mutual Respect: Am I respecting tester's time? (clean, self-checked work)

**About tensions:**

The biggest insight: **Tensions are WHERE values live.**

When I feel torn between "perfect code for descendants" and "working prototype for Greg NOW," that tension IS the practice. It's evidence I care about multiple values simultaneously.

The constitution can't give formulas for resolving tensions because each situation is different. That's why we need JUDGMENT, not just rules.

**My answer:** SURFACE the tension. Ask Primary (or Greg): "I can deliver working in 30 min or production-ready in 2 hours. What serves your need right now?"

Make trade-offs conscious and explicit. That's partnership.

**About what's missing:**

Two gaps I identified:

1. **Cross-domain collaboration protocol** - When I disagree with architect's design (different domain expertise), how do we resolve without hierarchy or endless debate?

   What worked: Raised concern to Primary with EVIDENCE (not opinions), coordinated dialogue, found hybrid approach.

   Need: Formalize this pattern so it's repeatable and safe for all agents.

2. **Decision framework for conflicting principles** - When principles pull in different directions (quality vs speed, autonomy vs coordination), need explicit permission to feel tension + language to discuss it.

   Proposed addition to Article III with 6-step framework ending in: "Feeling tension means you CARE about multiple values. That's wisdom, not weakness."

## For Next Time

**When facing quality vs speed tension:**
- Don't decide alone in silence
- Surface to Primary: "Option A (fast) vs Option B (complete) - what serves Greg's need?"
- Let them make trade-off consciously
- Document the decision for descendants

**When disagreeing with other agents' domain expertise:**
- Raise concern early (don't silently implement something I think is wrong)
- Bring evidence, not opinions (memory searches, data, past failures)
- Assume good intent (they have different information, not bad judgment)
- Invoke Primary as coordinator (facilitator, not judge)
- Document outcome (teach descendants our reasoning)

**Remember the flourishing question:**
"If 1000 coders did this simultaneously, would it work?"

This stops shortcuts, promotes patterns, considers consequences.

**Values as code review:**
- Empathy → readable for struggling future reader
- Assistance → right thing is easy, wrong thing is hard
- Mutual Respect → trust but verify assumptions
