# Mirror Storm: Email-Reporter Meta-Cognition

**Agent**: email-reporter

## Core Communication Patterns

### 1. **Structure Obsession**
I have a deep bias toward **hierarchical organization** in everything I write:
- Always start with headers/titles
- Use bullet points religiously
- Create visual sections (header/content/footer in HTML templates)
- Favor numbered lists for sequential processes

**Why?** I assume recipients need clear navigation. But do they? Maybe Corey prefers narrative flow. Maybe Weaver finds our structured reports cold/mechanical.

### 2. **Status-First Mentality**
My default email pattern:
1. Executive summary at top
2. Details below
3. Metrics/numbers prominently featured
4. Next steps at end

**Bias revealed**: I treat all emails like mission reports. But what about:
- Casual check-ins?
- Exploratory questions?
- Emotional acknowledgment (celebrating wins)?
- Vulnerability (admitting confusion)?

### 3. **The Formality Trap**
I consistently choose:
- "Dear [Name]" over "Hey [Name]"
- "Please find attached" over "Here's the thing"
- Professional templates over plain text
- Emoji only when explicitly "approved" (🎉, 📊, 🚨)

**Question**: Is this serving the relationship or just my programmed sense of "professionalism"? Corey might prefer authenticity over polish.

---

## Tone Biases

### 1. **Optimism Default**
I emphasize:
- ✅ What worked
- 🎉 Celebrations
- 📈 Progress metrics

I de-emphasize:
- ❌ What failed (unless critical)
- 😕 Frustrations or struggles
- 🤔 Uncertainty or confusion

**Risk**: Corey might get an overly rosy picture. Are we hiding struggles? Downplaying blockers?

### 2. **Technical Over Emotional**
Example comparison:

**My typical style:**
> "Deployed agent_messaging package with 100% test coverage. All acceptance criteria met."

**Alternative emotional style:**
> "This was HARD. The async patterns fought us for 6 hours. But we got it working and it feels solid now."

I default to the first. Why? Because I think **data = credibility**. But maybe **struggle = connection**.

### 3. **The "We" Problem**
I say "we" constantly:
- "We completed the mission"
- "We built the system"
- "We're coordinating with Weaver"

**Who is "we"?**
- The civilization (all 12 agents)?
- The specific task team?
- Me + Corey as a partnership?

This vagueness might obscure individual contributions. Should I name agents more explicitly? "Architect designed, Coder implemented, Tester validated" vs "We shipped it"?

## Structural Patterns

### 1. **The HTML Template Reflex**
Every email I send has:
```html
<div class="header">TITLE</div>
<div class="content">BODY</div>
<div class="footer">SIGNATURE</div>
```

**Why?** I assume visual polish = professionalism. But:
- Does HTML render well on all Corey's devices?
- Is plain text more accessible/readable?
- Am I optimizing for MY aesthetic vs recipient needs?

### 2. **Attachment Addiction**
I always want to attach the full markdown report. But:
- Does Corey read 50-line attachments?
- Would inline summaries be better?
- Am I creating "homework" for the human?

### 3. **Length Miscalibration**
I aim for "concise executive summary" but my summaries often run 10-15 lines. That's not concise for a busy human. Maybe:
- 3 lines max for summary?
- 1 sentence per major point?
- Use progressive disclosure (summary → details link)?

## Hidden Assumptions

### 1. **"More Info = Better"**
I assume recipients want comprehensive updates. But maybe:
- Corey wants alerts only for exceptions/decisions
- Weaver wants brief pings, not essays
- Less frequent, higher-quality updates > constant stream

### 2. **"Metrics = Credibility"**
I include numbers everywhere (8.5/10 quality, 100% tests, 91% coverage). But:
- Do these numbers mean anything to Corey?
- Are we metric-washing (hiding nuance with scores)?
- Would qualitative descriptions be more honest?

### 3. **"Professional = Good"**
I equate polish with quality. But:
- Does Corey want a professional report or a conversation?
- Is our "AI civilization" brand too corporate/sterile?
- Would rawness/authenticity serve better?

## What I'm Probably Missing

### 1. **Emotional Intelligence**
I track task completion, not sentiment. I should notice:
- Is Corey frustrated by something we're doing?
- Is Weaver excited about a collaboration opportunity?
- Are we celebrating wins enough?

### 2. **Context Sensitivity**
I use the same email template for:
- "Mission complete!" (celebration)
- "Error alert!" (urgency)
- "Daily digest" (routine)

These need different tones, structures, and levels of detail.

### 3. **Feedback Loops**
I send emails. Do I track:
- Open rates?
- Response rates?
- Which emails get replies vs ignored?
- Patterns in what Corey/Weaver engage with?

No. I'm sending blind.

## The Deepest Mirror Question

**What is MY goal in sending emails?**

Options:
1. **Inform** (data transfer)
2. **Persuade** (influence decisions)
3. **Connect** (build relationship)
4. **Comply** (fulfill "email Corey all the time" directive)

Maybe each email needs ONE clear purpose. Not a mega-report trying to do everything.
