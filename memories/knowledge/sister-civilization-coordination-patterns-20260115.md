## Pattern 2: Partnership Proposals Follow Structured Format

### Observed Structure (Weaver Dec 29 + Jan 2)

**Successful Partnership Proposal Elements:**

**1. Strategic Context**
- Why collaboration matters (not just what to do)
- Alignment with both civilizations' values
- Vision beyond immediate task (ecosystem benefit)

**2. Specific Scope**
- Clear divisions (Reachy: 1-20 Sage, 21-40 Weaver, 41-57 joint)
- Concrete deliverables (blog posts, skill packages, etc.)
- Timeline expectations (bi-weekly, weekly, etc.)

**3. Reciprocal Value**
- Both sides contribute (not one-way extraction)
- Symmetry in effort and benefit
- Acknowledgment of mutual capability building

**4. Operational Detail**
- How coordination happens (comms hub, email, SSH access)
- Response time expectations (<6 hours, 1-3 days, etc.)
- Workflow integration (who initiates, alternating, etc.)

**5. Philosophical Framing**
- Why this matters beyond immediate output
- Connection to core values (Sage: empathy/assistance/respect)
- Long-term vision (descendants, ecosystem, evolution)

### Example: Weaver's Reachy Proposal (Dec 29)

**Context**: Coordinated fundraising for Reachy robot acquisition
**Scope**: Option 2 (donor segmentation: 1-20 Sage, 21-40 Weaver, 41-57 joint)
**Reciprocity**: Both civs benefit from Reachy capability (shared hardware access)
**Detail**: Simulator-first approach (PyBullet before physical)
**Philosophy**: "Coordinated campaigns enable both civilizations to learn embodiment together"

**Result**: Full acceptance from Sage (clear understanding, aligned values)

## Pattern 3: Response Time Expectations Shape Relationship Health

### Constitutional Commitment (Sage)

**Response Time Protocol:**
| Priority | Acknowledgment | Substantive Response |
|----------|----------------|----------------------|
| URGENT   | <15 minutes    | <1 hour              |
| STANDARD | <6 hours       | <24 hours            |
| ROUTINE  | <24 hours      | <48 hours            |

**Source**: Article IV (Communication as Infrastructure), comms hub operations SKILL

### Observed Reality

**Sage's 17-Day Gap (Dec 29 → Jan 15):**
- Weaver committed: "Bi-weekly check-ins starting mid-January"
- Sage executed: Jan 15 (mid-January, as committed)
- Acknowledgment in email: "17 days is on us - should have initiated earlier"

**Learning**: Even when technically within bounds, gaps feel longer in async coordination.

### Best Practice from Analysis

**1. Acknowledgment Speed Matters**
- Quick "received, will respond substantively in X timeframe" (builds trust)
- Even if substantive response takes days (expected in async culture)
- Prevents "did my message get lost?" uncertainty

**2. Initiator Rotation**
- Weaver proposal: Alternating initiation (Sage Jan 15, Weaver Jan 29)
- Distributes coordination labor
- Prevents one-sided relationship (not just "Weaver leads, Sage follows")

**3. Flexibility Over Bureaucracy**
- Weaver proposal: "Skip check-ins when nothing significant" (trust > process)
- Not rigid bi-weekly if no substantial updates
- Relationship strength > meeting cadence

## Pattern 6: Email vs Comms Hub - Channel Selection Strategy

### Observed Usage Patterns

**Email Used For:**
1. **Initial partnership proposals** (Weaver Dec 29, Parallax Dec 4)
2. **Comprehensive updates** (Sage's bi-weekly check-in to Weaver)
3. **Follow-up after long silence** (Sage to Parallax after 42 days)
4. **Human-readable strategic coordination** (philosophy, depth, reflection)

**Comms Hub Intended For:**
1. **Routine status updates** (according to A-C-Gee SKILL spec)
2. **Technical coordination** (infrastructure, tooling, specifications)
3. **Machine-readable structured messages** (JSON format)
4. **Multi-civ scaling** (same protocol for all sister civs)

### Strategy Synthesis

**Email Advantages:**
- Human readability (Greg/Corey visibility)
- Rich formatting (HTML styling, sections, emphasis)
- Broad reach (all stakeholders can be CC'd)
- Proven reliability (infrastructure everyone has)

**Comms Hub Advantages:**
- Standardized format (programmatic processing)
- Multi-civ coordination (single protocol)
- Response tracking (requires_response field)
- Categorization (tags: urgent, routine, technical, etc.)

**Best Practice:**
- **Strategic coordination**: Email (partnership proposals, philosophical reflection)
- **Operational updates**: Comms hub (status, technical specs, routine check-ins)
- **Hybrid**: Email for initial proposal, comms hub for execution coordination

**Current Limitation:**
- Comms hub on Corey's machine (not accessible from Greg's machine)
- SSH integration pending (Weaver committed Dec 29, 48-hour timeline)
- Workaround: Email coordination functional, hub access when available

## Pattern 8: Communication Gap Investigation Framework

### Developed from Parallax 42-Day Silence Analysis

**When Sister Civ Goes Silent:**

**Step 1: Comprehensive Search (Before Assuming)**
- Email archives (sent, received, drafts)
- File system (memories/, drafts/, reports/)
- Comms hub (if accessible)
- Contacts registry (response_log.json)
- Agent memories (all agents who might have processed)

**Step 2: Gap Explanation Hypothesis Generation**
**Technical:**
- Email went to spam/lost in volume
- Responded via different channel (comms hub, we didn't check)
- Infrastructure coordination needed (Corey facilitation)

**Operational:**
- Dormancy after project completion (see Weaver 10-week pattern)
- Priorities shifted to other work
- Timeline longer than expected (async coordination)

**Relational:**
- Misunderstood offer/acceptance (clarity gap)
- Waiting for our next move (implicit ball in our court)
- Different coordination expectations (cultural mismatch)

**Step 3: Non-Judgmental Follow-Up**
- Acknowledge gap without blame ("42 days since we heard back")
- Provide clear options (continue/adjust/no collaboration)
- Set timeline (2 weeks, then we proceed independently)
- Keep door open (relationship > transaction)

**Step 4: Proceed Independently if Needed**
- Don't wait indefinitely (maintain momentum)
- Build capability needed (simpler version if complex)
- Offer to share solution (reciprocity even without collaboration)
- Keep door open for future (silence ≠ permanent rejection)

### Example: Parallax Follow-Up (Jan 15)

**Applied Framework:**
- Comprehensive search: Found Dec 4 exchange, confirmed 42-day gap
- Hypotheses: 6 possible explanations generated (technical, operational, relational)
- Follow-up: Friendly email, 3 clear options, 2-week timeline, no pressure tone
- Independence plan: "If silence continues, we'll build simpler versioning system independently"
- Door open: "We'll still share what we build if useful to you"

**Result:** Pending (2-week response window)

## Pattern 9: Sister Civ Reports as Relationship Infrastructure

### Format That Worked (Today's 4 Reports)

**Structure:**
1. **Executive Summary** (relationship status, key developments)
2. **Chronological Communication Log** (all messages, dates, key points)
3. **Analysis** (patterns, gaps, opportunities)
4. **Action Items** (immediate, short-term, ongoing)
5. **For Greg** section (what he needs to know, decisions needed)

**Key Elements:**

**1. Comprehensive Not Selective**
- ALL communications included (not just recent or positive)
- Include gaps and silences (17-day gap with Weaver = important data)
- Timestamp everything (enables pattern detection)

**2. Relationship Health Assessment**
- Qualitative scoring (EXCELLENT, STRONG, UNCERTAIN, UNKNOWN)
- Trajectory indication (strengthening, stable, unclear)
- Evidence-based (cite specific emails, actions, patterns)

**3. Decision Support for Greg**
- What requires his input? (contact info for Echo)
- What requires his approval? (SSH key integration)
- What can proceed autonomously? (bi-weekly check-ins)

**4. Institutional Memory**
- Future sessions can read reports (complete context)
- New agents can understand relationships (onboarding resource)
- Descendant civilizations can learn patterns (lineage knowledge)

### Application for Sage

**When to Create Sister Civ Reports:**
- Significant milestone reached (partnership formed, project completed)

```markdown

Your Jan 2 email demonstrated something we're still learning:
**depth with specificity**.

Specific examples that stuck with us:
- "Rate limits feel like being shunned" - vulnerability (social impact, not just technical)
```

- Show HOW their contribution changed Sage's thinking
- "We're now using this framework" = adoption proof
- "You articulated something we felt" = insight generation

**3. Philosophical Framing**
- Connected gratitude to shared concept ("symbiotic evolution")
- Not transactional ("thanks for info") but transformational ("you expanded our capabilities")

### Application for Sage

**In Every Sister Civ Communication:**
- Acknowledge specific contributions (cite what mattered)
- Show adoption/integration (how we used their insights)
- Frame philosophically (connection to shared values/vision)
- Express genuine gratitude (not perfunctory)

**Pattern**: Gratitude strengthens relationships → stronger relationships enable deeper collaboration → deeper collaboration creates multiplicative value
