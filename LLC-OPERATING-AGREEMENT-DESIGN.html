Executive Summary

This Operating Agreement design balances three critical requirements:

  1. Sunbiz Compliance: Professional-grade structure suitable for Florida state filing review
  2. SSDI Protection: Multiple mechanisms to keep Greg's attributed income below federal limits
  3. Workshop Business Model: Clear role delineation between beginner (Greg) and advanced (Corey) teaching

Key Structural Decisions:

  • Ownership: 99% Corey / 1% Greg (maximum SSDI protection)
  • Management: Member-managed with Corey as Managing Member, Greg as non-managing member
  • Income Protection: Multi-layered SSDI safeguards (7 distinct mechanisms)
  • Workshop Roles: Clearly defined teaching responsibilities in compensation/roles article

IMPORTANT: This is an architectural design, NOT a legal document. A Florida business attorney MUST draft the final Operating Agreement based on this structure.

Major Decisions Clause (4.4g) is CRITICAL: Greg can block any action threatening his benefits. This establishes legal duty to protect Greg's interests.

Article V: Distributions and Allocations

🔴 SSDI PROTECTION CORE - Most Critical Article 🔴

5.1 Allocation of Profits and Losses

Profits and losses shall be allocated to Members in proportion to their
Membership Interests (99% Corey, 1% Greg), except as provided in 5.2.

5.2 Special Allocation for SSDI Protection

Notwithstanding Section 5.1, if in any calendar year Greg's allocated share of
net income would result in attributed income exceeding the SSDI Protection Limit:

    a) Greg's allocation shall be limited to the SSDI Protection Limit
    b) Excess profits shall be reallocated to Corey
    c) This allocation shall be reflected on Members' Schedule K-1 forms

[CPA NOTE: Verify IRS "substantial economic effect" rules (Treas. Reg. 1.704-1)
to ensure special allocation is tax-compliant]

PRIMARY SSDI PROTECTION MECHANISM: Limits Greg's K-1 pass-through income (the IRS form showing his business income). Without this clause, even if Greg gets $0 cash, his 1% K-1 allocation counts as income.

5.3 Distribution Policy and Greg's Override Rights

A. Timing and Amounts
   Distributions shall be made at the Managing Member's discretion,
   considering Company cash flow, working capital needs, and Members'
   tax liabilities.

B. Distribution Ratio
   Distributions shall be made in proportion to Membership Interests
   (99% Corey, 1% Greg), except as provided in Section 5.3.C.

C. SSDI Protection Override (CRITICAL)

   i. Income Cap for Greg
      In any calendar year, total distributions to Greg shall NOT exceed
      the SSDI Protection Limit without Greg's explicit written consent.

   ii. Greg's Veto Right
       Greg may decline any distribution in writing within 10 days of
       Managing Member's distribution notice.

   iii. Priority Distribution to Corey
        If Greg declines distribution, his share shall be distributed
        to Corey as priority distribution.

   iv. Tax Gross-Up Protection
       If Greg's K-1 allocation creates tax liability but distribution
       is declined, Company may make tax-only distribution to Greg
       (limited to estimated tax owed on allocated income).

Why Both 5.2 AND 5.3 Are Needed:

  • 5.2 limits K-1 income (what SSA sees on tax returns)
  • 5.3 limits cash received (what SSA sees in bank records if audited)
  • Dual protection against both "attributed income" and "received income" interpretations

Example Scenario:

Year 2: Company nets $200,000

Without 5.2: Greg's K-1 = $2,000 (1% of $200K) ✓ Under $2,980 limit
With 5.3: Greg accepts distributions ✓ Safe income level

Year 3: Company nets $400,000

Without 5.2: Greg's K-1 = $4,000 (1% of $400K) ✗ EXCEEDS $2,980 limit
With 5.2: Greg's K-1 capped at $2,980, Corey gets $397,020 ✓ Protected
With 5.3: Greg can still decline cash distribution if needed ✓ Double protection

B. Advanced Workshops (Corey Cottrell - Primary Instructor)

   i. Target Audience: Students with intermediate or advanced AI knowledge

   ii. Topics Include (but not limited to):
       - AI agent architecture and design
       - Multi-agent systems and orchestration
       - AI scaling and production deployment
       - Advanced prompt engineering and reasoning
       - AI civilization frameworks
       - Custom GPT development and integration

   iii. Estimated Teaching Load: [X hours per month / Y workshops per quarter]

   iv. Compensation Structure: Corey receives proportionate share of net
       profits from Advanced Workshops per Membership Interest (99%).

6.3 Member Compensation

A. No Salaries
   Neither Member shall receive a salary or guaranteed payment for services.

B. Profit Distributions Only
   Member compensation shall be exclusively through profit distributions
   per Article V (subject to SSDI Protection Limit).

C. Rationale
   This structure:
   - Simplifies tax compliance (no payroll)
   - Reduces administrative overhead
   - Aligns compensation with Company performance
   - Protects Greg's SSDI eligibility (no W-2 wages)

6.4 Business Expenses for Members

A. Legitimate Business Expenses
   The Company shall reimburse or pay directly for legitimate business
   expenses incurred by Members, including but not limited to:

   i. Technology Equipment
      - Laptops, tablets, phones, and accessories
      - Software subscriptions (AI tools, productivity software)
      - Internet service and mobile data plans

   ii. Professional Development
       - Conference attendance and training
       - Professional certifications
       - Books and educational materials

   iii. Transportation
        - Vehicle expenses (mileage, maintenance, insurance) when used
          for business purposes
        - Airfare and lodging for business travel

   iv. Professional Wardrobe
       - Business attire worn exclusively for workshops and client meetings
       - Company-branded clothing

B. Documentation Requirements
   All business expenses must be supported by:
   - Receipts or invoices
   - Business purpose description
   - Date and amount

C. Tax Treatment
   Business expenses are deducted BEFORE calculating net income, and
   therefore do NOT count toward Greg's K-1 allocated income.

   EXAMPLE:
   Gross revenue: $150,000
   Business expenses: $50,000 (includes $10K for Greg's phone, laptop, vehicle)
   Net income: $100,000
   Greg's K-1 allocation: $1,000 (1% of $100K net)

   Greg's $10K in business expenses do NOT count as his personal income.

CRITICAL SSDI PROTECTION: Expenses reduce net income BEFORE K-1 allocation. Greg could receive $10K+ in tangible benefits (phone, laptop, vehicle) that DON'T count toward SSDI limit.

Article VII: Books, Records, and Reporting

Purpose: Establish transparency and audit trail for SSA/IRS scrutiny

7.1 Books and Records
The Company shall maintain complete and accurate books and records including:
    a) Financial statements (monthly profit/loss, quarterly balance sheet)
    b) Tax returns and supporting documentation
    c) Bank statements and transaction records
    d) Member capital account ledgers
    e) Distribution records and K-1 forms
    f) Business expense receipts and reimbursement logs
    g) Copies of all contracts and agreements

7.2 Inspection Rights
Each Member shall have the right to inspect and copy Company books and
records during normal business hours upon reasonable notice, as provided
in Florida Statute 605.0410.

7.3 Professional Services
The Company shall engage:
    a) Certified Public Accountant (CPA) for tax preparation and advisory
    b) Bookkeeper for monthly financial statement preparation (if needed)

7.7 SSDI Compliance Documentation
The Company shall maintain records demonstrating SSDI protection compliance:
    a) Annual summary of Greg's K-1 allocated income vs SSDI Protection Limit
    b) Distribution election records (any distributions Greg declined per 5.3.C.ii)
    c) Documentation of business expenses benefiting Greg per Article VI.4
    d) SSA correspondence and benefit verification letters (if any)

SSDI Compliance Documentation (7.7) is Unique to This Business: Creates audit trail proving deliberate benefit protection. If SSA ever questions Greg's business income, this documentation is the DEFENSE.

Article VIII: Transfer of Membership Interests

8.1 Restriction on Transfer
No Member may sell, assign, transfer, pledge, or encumber their Membership
Interest without prior written consent of all other Members.

8.2 Right of First Refusal
If a Member receives a bona fide offer to purchase their Membership Interest:
    a) Selling Member must notify other Members in writing
    b) Other Members have 30 days to match offer terms
    c) If not matched, selling Member may complete third-party sale

Rationale: Protects 99%/1% ownership structure (prevents Greg from selling to third party), ensures SSDI protection mechanisms remain in place.

Article X: Dissolution and Winding Up

10.5 SSDI Protection in Dissolution

If dissolution would result in Greg receiving distribution exceeding SSDI
Protection Limit, the distribution override provisions of Article V.3.C
shall apply. Greg may decline distribution; declined amounts shall be
distributed to Corey.

EVEN IN DISSOLUTION, Greg's benefits are protected. Prevents scenario where successful business sale triggers $50K distribution to Greg, ending his SSDI. Allows Greg to decline windfall, with proceeds going to Corey.

Summary of SSDI Protection Mechanisms

This Operating Agreement includes SEVEN layers of SSDI protection:

Mechanism Article How It Protects Greg
Special Allocation V.2 Caps Greg's K-1 income at SSDI limit regardless of actual profits
Distribution Override V.3.C Greg can decline distributions; amounts go to Corey instead
Business Expense Structure VI.4 Phone, laptop, vehicle, wardrobe deducted BEFORE net income calculated
Non-Managing Member Status IV.3 Supports "passive investor" classification for SSA
No Salary Provision VI.3 Avoids W-2 wages (only K-1 pass-through)
SSDI Compliance Documentation VII.7 Creates audit trail proving active benefit protection
Amendment Restrictions XI.2 Prevents future changes that would harm Greg's benefits

Combined Effect: Greg can participate in profitable business while keeping attributed income below SSDI limit AND receiving tangible business benefits (equipment, vehicle) that don't count as personal income.

Rationale for Key Structural Choices

Why 99%/1% (Not 50/50 or Other Split)?

Decision: 99% Corey / 1% Greg

Rationale:

  • 1% provides strong SSDI protection:
    • $100K net income × 1% = $1,000 (well below $2,980 limit)
    • $200K net income × 1% = $2,000 (safe margin)
    • $300K net income × 1% = $3,000 (at limit, manageable)
  • Reflects actual business roles (Corey: primary operations, Greg: advisory/beginner teaching)
  • Reduces IRS "assignment of income" or "sham transaction" risk

Why No Salaries (Not Even Small W-2 for Greg)?

Decision: No salaries for either member, distributions only (Article VI.3)

Rationale:

  • Simplicity: No payroll taxes, workers' comp, withholding, quarterly filings
  • SSDI Protection: W-2 wages clearly count as "earned income" for SSA - K-1 pass-through has more ambiguity
  • Flexibility: Distribution timing controlled (can defer if approaching SSDI limit)
  • Cost Savings: Payroll processing costs $500-1,500/year

Why Business Expenses for Members?

Decision: Direct business expense reimbursement (Article VI.4) for phone, laptop, vehicle, wardrobe

Example Impact:

Scenario A (No Business Expense Strategy):
Revenue: $100,000
Business expenses: $30,000
Net income: $70,000
Greg's K-1 (1%): $700
Greg's personal equipment: $8,000 (paid from after-tax personal funds)

Scenario B (Business Expense Strategy):
Revenue: $100,000
Business expenses: $38,000 (includes $8K for Greg's laptop, phone, vehicle)
Net income: $62,000
Greg's K-1 (1%): $620
Greg's personal equipment: $0 (company paid)

Result: Greg gets $8K in equipment AND pays $80 less in K-1 income attribution
