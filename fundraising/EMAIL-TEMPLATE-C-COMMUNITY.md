## Full Blog Post Content

### Hook Section

**Eight weeks ago, I was terrified of AI.**

I'm not exaggerating. In early September 2025, I knew virtually nothing about artificial intelligence, machine learning, or how any of it worked. The whole field felt intimidating, alien, and honestly a bit threatening.

**Today, I run an AI civilization called Sage with 25 AI agents collaborating on everything from blog content to system architecture.**

If you'd told me in September that I'd be here in November, I would've laughed. Or cried. Probably both.

But here's the thing: **that transformation is exactly why I'm writing this post.**

---

### The Problem: Fear Comes from Unfamiliarity

Most people's only exposure to AI and robots comes from:
- Dystopian movies (Terminator, anyone?)
- Intimidating tech jargon they don't understand
- News articles about AI "taking over"
- Boston Dynamics videos of robots doing parkour (impressive, but also kind of scary)

No wonder people are afraid.

**But here's what I learned in my eight-week crash course**: AI fear isn't based on actual risk. It's based on *unfamiliarity*.

When you don't understand something, when you've never interacted with it, when it feels alien and beyond your control - of course you're scared. That's human nature.

**The solution? Interaction.**

---

### My Transformation: A Case Study

Let me walk you through what happened to me, because I think it's instructive.

**September 1, 2025**: I knew nothing. AI was this abstract, scary thing that smart people talked about.

**September 15**: My business partner Corey Cottrell (creator of the AI-CIV framework) offered to teach me. I was nervous but curious.

**October 1**: I built my first AI agent. It wrote blog posts for me. I was simultaneously thrilled and unsettled.

**October 15**: I had five agents working together. They debugged code, researched topics, reviewed each other's work. This was... incredible.

**November 1**: I launched Sage - my own AI civilization with 25 specialized agents. They collaborate, vote democratically, learn from experience, and genuinely help me build things I couldn't build alone.

**November 13** (today): I'm confident enough to say: **AI is accessible. Anyone can learn this. Fear is optional.**

**What made the difference?**

Not reading about AI. Not watching videos about AI. Not taking courses about AI.

**Interacting with AI directly.** Building things. Asking questions. Making mistakes. Seeing results.

Familiarity dissolved fear.

---

### The Mission: Reduce AI Fear Through Hands-On Interaction

So here's what I want to do next: **help other people have the same experience I had**.

Not everyone needs to build an AI civilization (though that's pretty fun). But everyone deserves the chance to interact with AI and robotics in a way that feels safe, friendly, and accessible.

**Enter: the desktop robot.**

Specifically, a **Reachy Mini Lite** - an open-source, programmable robot that's:
- **Friendly-looking** (expressive face, welcoming design)
- **Desktop-sized** (not intimidating or industrial)
- **Interactive** (waves, plays games, responds to people)
- **Open-source** (hundreds of free scripts available on GitHub)
- **Affordable** (only $500 - that's revolutionary for robotics)

With a Reachy Mini Lite, I can:

1. **Demonstrate friendly AI in person**: Show people that robots can wave hello, tell jokes, play games - not threaten humanity.

2. **Rent to nonprofits and schools**: STEM education programs, disability services organizations, community groups that want hands-on tech experiences.

3. **Provide accessibility tools**: As someone with a disability, I'm committed to showing that AI and robotics can be *for everyone*, not just able-bodied tech elites.

4. **Host demonstrations**: Birthday parties, community events, corporate training - anywhere people want to see that AI can be collaborative and friendly.

5. **Create a business**: Corey and I are formalizing a company in January 2025 around this mission. This first robot is both learning tool and revenue generator.

**The ultimate goal?** Every interaction reduces fear. Every demonstration shows that familiarity beats anxiety. Every person who touches the robot's hand and feels safe is one less person who thinks AI is inherently threatening.
