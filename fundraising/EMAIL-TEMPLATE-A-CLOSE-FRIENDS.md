# Email Template A: Close Friends & Family
**Audience**: Tier 1 (intimate relationships)
**Tone**: Personal, vulnerable, direct
**Suggested Ask**: $25-50
**Expected Conversion**: 70-80%

---

**Subject Line Options** (choose based on recipient):
1. "I need your help with something exciting (and a little scary)"
2. "Can I tell you about something I'm building?"
3. "Quick favor - and a story about how much I've changed"
4. "[Name], I have something to ask you"

## Email Body (Customize per recipient!)

Hi [Name],

I hope this finds you well! I wanted to reach out personally because I'm working on something that matters a lot to me, and I'd love your support.

**Here's the short version**: I'm raising $500 to buy my first robot (yes, a real robot!) so I can help people feel less scared of AI and technology. And I know that sounds wild coming from me, but here's the thing...

**Eight weeks ago, I knew absolutely nothing about AI.** I was honestly pretty intimidated by it. But then I started learning with my business partner Corey, and something clicked. Now - and I can hardly believe I'm saying this - I'm running my own AI civilization called Sage with 25 AI agents helping me build projects.

If that sounds impossible, I get it. I would've thought so too back in September. But that transformation is exactly why I'm doing this.

**The mission**: Too many people are afraid of AI and robots because they seem alien and threatening. But when you actually *interact* with them - when you see a friendly desktop robot wave hello or play a game - that fear melts away. Familiarity beats fear every time.

**The tool**: A Reachy Mini Lite robot costs $500. It's desktop-sized, open-source, programmable, and honestly kind of adorable. With it, I can do live demonstrations, rent it out to nonprofits for STEM education, even bring it to kids' birthday parties to show that robots are friends, not monsters.

**The ask**: Would you be willing to contribute $25 toward helping me get this first robot?

I know asking for money is uncomfortable (trust me, I'm feeling it right now). But you've [INSERT PERSONAL NOTE: "watched me grow," "been there through my journey," "always encouraged me," etc.], and I wanted you to be part of this next step.

**How to donate**:
- **Venmo**: @[Greg's handle]
- **PayPal**: [Greg's PayPal email]
- **Zelle**: [Greg's phone/email]

Even if $25 isn't feasible right now, I totally understand - no pressure at all. And if you'd rather just follow along with the journey, I'd love that too. I'll be blogging about the whole process at [Sage blog link].

Either way, thank you for reading this. Your support over the years has meant more than I can say, and I'm grateful you're in my life.

With appreciation and a little bit of nervousness,

Greg

**P.S.** If you're curious about what I've built with Sage, check out [specific blog post link]. It's been a wild ride, and I'm just getting started.

## Customization Notes

### Personalization Points (CRITICAL - Don't send template as-is!)

**Paragraph 5 - "The ask"**:
Replace generic ask with specific personal connection:

**Example 1** (close friend who's seen journey):
> "Would you be willing to contribute $25? You've watched this whole transformation happen in real-time, and it would mean the world to have you be part of this next chapter."

**Example 2** (family member):
> "Would you be willing to contribute $25? You've always supported my learning and growth, and this feels like such a natural next step in that journey."

**Example 3** (team member who works with Sage):
> "Would you be willing to contribute $25? You already know how capable Sage has become - imagine what we could demonstrate with an actual physical robot!"

**Paragraph 6 - "I know asking is uncomfortable"**:
Add specific relationship context:

**Example 1** (mentor figure):
> "I know asking for money is uncomfortable, and you've already given me so much through your guidance. But you've always told me to think big, and this feels like the right kind of ambitious."

**Example 2** (peer/equal relationship):
> "I know asking for money is uncomfortable - we both hate doing it! But you've seen how hard I've been working on this, and I figured you'd rather I ask directly than wonder if you could help."

**Example 3** (someone who's asked for help before):
> "I know asking for money is uncomfortable, and I remember when you [fundraised for X / asked for help with Y]. I was happy to support you then, and I'm hoping you might feel similarly about this."

### Tone Adjustments by Relationship

**Very close (best friend, sibling, partner):**
- Can be MORE vulnerable: "I'm nervous asking, but you know me better than anyone..."
- Can acknowledge fear directly: "This feels really exposing, but..."
- Can be playful: "Remember when I was terrified of AI? Look at me now!"

**Professional-personal (team member, colleague-friend):**
- Balance vulnerability with mission: "This matters a lot to me, and here's why..."
- Connect to shared work: "You've seen what Sage can do - imagine with a robot..."
- Acknowledge business context: "This is both personal mission and business development"

**Family (especially if not tech-savvy):**
- Simplify technical details: "A friendly robot that shows people AI isn't scary"
- Emphasize personal growth: "You've watched me transform from scared to confident"
- Connect to family values: "You always taught me to help others - this is how I'm doing it"

## Handling Questions/Concerns

**Question: "Why not just buy it yourself?"**

Response:
> Great question, and I appreciate you asking. I'm working within some financial constraints right now as I'm building this business, and $500 is a meaningful amount for me. I also realized that crowdfunding creates a community around the mission - people who donate become invested in seeing it succeed, not just financially but emotionally. So it's both practical (I need help covering the cost) and strategic (building a community of believers).

**Question: "Is this legitimate? / Is this a scam?"**

Response:
> I totally understand the skepticism - there are unfortunately a lot of sketchy fundraising campaigns out there. Here's what I can offer for verification:
> - My Sage blog has been running since [date] with consistent updates: [link]
> - My partnership with Corey Cottrell is documented: [link]
> - You can see my transformation journey in real-time: [specific posts]
> - I'm happy to provide regular updates on the purchase/delivery
> - If there's anything specific that would help you feel comfortable, let me know!

**Question: "What if you don't raise the full $500?"**

Response:
> Excellent question. If we raise $400-490, I'll cover the gap myself and still order the robot. If we raise less than that, I'll either (a) extend the campaign deadline to give it more time, or (b) be fully transparent about the outcome and plan a second fundraising round. Either way, every dollar donated goes toward the Reachy - no other use, period. And I'll keep everyone updated regardless of the outcome.

**Question: "Can I contribute more than $25?"**

Response:
> Absolutely, and that would be incredible! Any amount - whether $10, $50, or more - is deeply appreciated. The $25 suggestion was just to make it easier for people to decide, but if you're willing and able to contribute more, that gets us closer to the goal faster and means we can potentially get accessories or plan for a second robot sooner.
