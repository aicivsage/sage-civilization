## 🚨 ISSUE #1: Identify the True $3,000 Income Limit Source

### The Problem
Your business plan mentions a **$3,000/year income limit**, but federal SSDI allows ~$18,600/year (Substantial Gainful Activity threshold). This discrepancy must be resolved before forming ANY business entity.

### Most Likely Source
**Section 8 Housing Voucher** - NOT SSDI

- Section 8 has much stricter earned income limits than SSDI
- Section 8 rules vary by local housing authority
- Forming an LLC without understanding Section 8 rules could trigger voucher loss

### What You Must Do

**CRITICAL ACTIONS (Complete BEFORE LLC formation):**

1. **Locate Section 8 paperwork**
   - Find your housing voucher income reporting requirements
   - Identify exact earned income limits
   - Look for self-employment income treatment guidance

2. **Contact Section 8 caseworker directly**
   - Ask: "How is self-employment income counted toward my voucher eligibility?"
   - Ask: "Can I deduct business expenses before counting income?"
   - Ask: "What happens if I own 1% of an LLC but only receive $2,000/year in distributions?"

3. **Review SSDI award letter**
   - Confirm your actual SGA threshold
   - Look for work incentive programs (Ticket to Work, PASS, etc.)
   - Verify whether you're in Trial Work Period

**Why This Matters:**
- Wrong assumption = wrong business structure
- Section 8 loss could be catastrophic (housing stability)
- SSDI rules are more forgiving than Section 8

**Timeline:** COMPLETE THIS FIRST - Do not proceed to Issue #2 until resolved

## 🚨 ISSUE #2: Draft Operating Agreement with Protective Provisions

### The Problem
A standard LLC operating agreement won't protect your benefits. You need **custom provisions** that legally cap your income while maintaining your ownership stake.

### What the Operating Agreement Must Include

**Section 1: Unequal Ownership Structure**
```
Members and Capital Contributions:
- Greg Smithwick: 1% ownership interest
- Corey Cottrell: 99% ownership interest
```

**Section 2: Income Distribution Caps (CRITICAL)**
```
Protective Distribution Language:

"Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, distributions
to Member Greg Smithwick shall be limited to a maximum of $2,500 per
calendar year, or such lesser amount as Greg Smithwick determines necessary
to maintain eligibility for Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)
and/or Section 8 housing assistance.

Greg Smithwick shall have sole discretion to decline any distribution that
would jeopardize benefit eligibility. Any distributions declined by Greg
Smith shall be redirected to Corey Cottrell as priority distributions.

This provision may only be amended with unanimous member consent AND
written verification from a disability benefits attorney that the
amendment will not jeopardize Greg Smithwick's benefits."
```

**Section 3: Business Expense Provisions**
```
Business Expenses for Greg Smithwick's Role:

The LLC may pay directly for Greg Smithwick's legitimate business expenses
including but not limited to:
- Cell phone and service plan
- Laptop and software
- Vehicle mileage or lease (business use portion)
- Professional development and training
- Health insurance (if allowed by plan rules)

These direct payments by the LLC do NOT count as distributions to Greg
Smith and do not count toward the $2,500 annual distribution cap.
```

**Section 4: Override Rights**
```
Greg Smithwick retains absolute right to refuse any distribution,
compensation, or benefit that could jeopardize his disability benefits
or housing assistance.
```

### What You Must Do

**Option A: Free/Low-Cost (Interim)**
- Use online LLC operating agreement template (LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer)
- Manually add the protective provisions listed above
- Have Corey review for clarity
- **Risk:** Not attorney-reviewed, may have gaps

**Option B: Professional (When Business Has Revenue)**
- Pay disability benefits attorney to draft custom agreement
- Cost: $500-1,500
- **Benefit:** Legally airtight, specifically tailored to your situation

**Recommended Approach:** Start with Option A, upgrade to Option B when business generates revenue

**Timeline:** Draft operating agreement BEFORE filing LLC formation documents

## 🚨 ISSUE #3: Understand Business Income vs. Wage Distinction

### The Problem
How you receive money from the business affects whether it counts toward your benefit limits.

### Three Types of Business Income

**Type 1: Distributions (LLC Profits)**
- Money paid to you as owner based on your 1% ownership
- Counts toward SSDI SGA threshold
- May count differently for Section 8 (verify with caseworker)
- **Strategy:** Cap at $2,500/year maximum

**Type 2: Wages (W-2 Salary)**
- If LLC elects S-Corp tax treatment, you'd need "reasonable compensation" as W-2 wages
- Wages count 100% toward SSDI and Section 8 limits
- **Strategy:** AVOID W-2 wages entirely (stay as LLC-partnership, don't elect S-Corp)

**Type 3: Business Expense Reimbursements**
- LLC pays directly for your phone, laptop, vehicle mileage, etc.
- **These do NOT count as your income** (they're business expenses)
- **Strategy:** Maximize legitimate business expenses paid directly by LLC

### What You Must Do

**Action 1: Clarify with SSA**
- Call SSA (1-800-772-1213)
- Ask: "If I own 1% of an LLC and receive $2,500/year in distributions, how does that affect my SSDI?"
- Ask: "If the LLC pays my cell phone bill as a business expense, does that count as my income?"

**Action 2: Clarify with Section 8**
- Ask your caseworker the same questions about distributions vs. business expenses
- Get answers IN WRITING if possible

**Action 3: Structure Your Role**
- Title: "Strategic Advisor" (not active manager)
- Minimal hours per week (5-10 hours advisory work)
- Legitimate business expenses: phone, laptop, mileage for client meetings
- Corey does all active management, client delivery, operations

**Why This Matters:**
- $10,000 in distributions = benefit loss
- $10,000 in business expenses paid by LLC = no impact on benefits
- Strategic structuring can mean the difference between viable business and benefit loss

**Timeline:** Clarify BEFORE first LLC distribution or expense reimbursement

## 🚨 ISSUE #4: Business Expense Documentation and Substantiation

### The Problem
If you claim expenses don't count as your income, you must PROVE they're legitimate business expenses with proper documentation.

### IRS Business Expense Test (Three Requirements)

An expense is deductible as a business expense if it's:

1. **Ordinary** - Common and accepted in your industry
2. **Necessary** - Helpful and appropriate for your business
3. **Reasonable** - Not excessive or extravagant

**Examples for AI consulting business:**

✅ **Legitimate Business Expenses (Don't Count as Your Income)**
- Cell phone and monthly service ($50-100/month) - client communication
- Laptop ($1,000-1,500) - necessary for AI development work
- Vehicle mileage to client meetings ($0.67/mile in 2024) - business travel
- Professional subscriptions (Claude Pro, ChatGPT, AI tools) - industry tools
- Business insurance - professional liability
- Co-working space membership - professional meeting space
- Professional development - AI conferences, courses

❌ **Personal Expenses (NOT Deductible)**
- Groceries, entertainment, personal shopping
- Personal vehicle registration/insurance (only business use % is deductible)
- Vacations disguised as "business trips"
- Excessive luxury purchases

### What You Must Do

**Documentation Requirements (DAY ONE of LLC):**

1. **Separate business bank account**
   - Open LLC business checking account immediately after formation
   - ALL business expenses paid from this account
   - ALL distributions paid from this account
   - NEVER comingle personal and business funds

2. **Expense tracking system**
   - Use QuickBooks Self-Employed or Wave (free) for expense tracking
   - Every expense must have:
     - Date
     - Amount
     - Business purpose description
     - Receipt/invoice (keep PDF copies)
   - Example entry: "12/5/2025 - $89.99 - Cell phone bill - Client communication and project coordination"

3. **Mileage log (if claiming vehicle expenses)**
   - Date, starting odometer, ending odometer
   - Purpose of trip
   - Client or business destination
   - Use app like MileIQ or Everlance (automates tracking)

4. **Written policies in Operating Agreement**
   - "LLC shall reimburse or pay directly for members' reasonable and necessary business expenses"
   - "Members must submit expense reports with receipts within 30 days"
   - "Reimbursements require approval by managing member (Corey)"

### Why This Matters

**If audited by IRS:**
- Poor documentation = expense disallowed = counts as your income
- Good documentation = expense approved = doesn't count as income

**If reviewed by SSA or Section 8:**
- You must prove expenses were business-related, not personal
- Burden of proof is on you
- "I think my phone is for business" ≠ sufficient proof
- Detailed logs + receipts = strong proof

**Timeline:** Set up expense tracking system BEFORE LLC receives first dollar of revenue

## 🚨 ISSUE #5: Professional Review Requirement and Risk Mitigation

### The Problem
You cannot afford an attorney or CPA right now, but forming an LLC without professional review carries risks.

### Specific Risks You're Accepting

**Risk 1: Operating Agreement Gaps**
- Template operating agreement may not include all necessary protective provisions
- Missing language could allow distributions that jeopardize benefits
- Unclear member rights could cause disputes with Corey

**Risk 2: Tax Election Mistakes**
- Filing wrong IRS forms could trigger wrong tax treatment
- Could end up with S-Corp treatment (requires W-2 wages) instead of partnership
- Wrong 1099/K-1 reporting could trigger SSA or Section 8 review

**Risk 3: Benefit Termination**
- One mistake (excess distribution, wrong income reporting) could trigger benefit review
- Benefit loss process is slow to reverse (months to appeal)
- Housing voucher loss = immediate housing instability

**Risk 4: IRS Audit Exposure**
- Home business deductions are high audit risk
- Aggressive expense claims without documentation = penalties
- Incorrect tax forms = IRS notices

### Mitigation Strategy (Zero-Budget Approach)

**Phase 1: Free Self-Education (NOW - Before LLC Formation)**

1. **IRS Resources (Free)**
   - Publication 334 "Tax Guide for Small Business"
   - Publication 535 "Business Expenses"
   - Publication 463 "Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses"
   - Download at IRS.gov/forms

2. **SSA Resources (Free)**
   - Red Book "Guide to Work Incentives" (SSA.gov)
   - Call SSA Work Incentives Planning (1-866-968-7842) - FREE
   - Ask about PASS (Plan to Achieve Self-Support) program

3. **Section 8 Guidance (Free)**
   - HUD Housing Choice Voucher Guidebook
   - Your local housing authority's earned income policy
   - Schedule meeting with your Section 8 caseworker (request in writing)

4. **Florida LLC Formation Guide (Free)**
   - Florida Division of Corporations website
   - Step-by-step LLC formation instructions
   - Operating agreement templates (generic, but free)

**Phase 2: Partial Professional Consultation (When Urgent)**

If you MUST form LLC before business has revenue:

**Option A: Limited Scope Attorney Consult**
- Cost: $200-400 for 1-2 hour consultation
- Focus: Review your self-drafted operating agreement
- Ask: "Does this language protect my SSDI and Section 8?"
- Don't ask for full drafting (too expensive)

**Option B: Disability Benefits Attorney (One Question)**
- Many offer free initial consultation
- Ask ONE critical question: "Can I own 1% of an LLC receiving $2,500/year distributions without jeopardizing SSDI?"
- Get answer in writing if possible

**Option C: SCORE Mentorship (Free)**
- SCORE.org offers free small business mentorship
- Find mentor with LLC formation experience
- Ask them to review your operating agreement language
- **Note:** They're not lawyers, but can spot obvious problems

**Phase 3: Full Professional Review (When Business Has Revenue)**

Once LLC generates revenue, use business income to pay:

**Priority 1: Disability Benefits Attorney**
- Cost: $500-1,500
- Review LLC structure, operating agreement
- Confirm benefit protection strategy is sound
- Get written opinion for your records (valuable if ever audited by SSA)

**Priority 2: CPA Experienced with SSDI**
- Cost: $500-1,000 for tax planning consultation
- Review expense documentation system
- Advise on tax election strategy
- Prepare first year's tax returns (LLC + personal)

**Priority 3: Business Attorney (Ongoing)**
- Cost: $1,500-3,000 annually
- Client contracts and terms of service
- Liability protection review
- Operating agreement amendments as business grows

### What You Must Do

**Immediate (Before LLC Formation):**

☐ Read IRS Pub 334, 535 (business expenses)
☐ Read SSA Red Book (work incentives)
☐ Call Section 8 caseworker (income rules)
☐ Call SSA Work Incentives Planning (PASS program eligibility)
☐ Find SCORE mentor for operating agreement review

**Within 30 Days of LLC Formation:**

☐ Open business bank account
☐ Set up QuickBooks or Wave accounting
☐ Create mileage tracking system
☐ File LLC Annual Report (Florida - due by May 1 each year)

**Within 90 Days of First Revenue:**

☐ Schedule disability benefits attorney consultation ($500-1,500)
☐ Schedule CPA consultation for tax planning ($500-1,000)
☐ Get written confirmation that structure protects benefits

### Risk Acceptance Statement

By forming an LLC without full professional review, you accept these risks:

- Operating agreement may have gaps
- Tax reporting may have errors (fixable, but could trigger reviews)
- Benefit protection strategy may be incomplete
- You may need to amend LLC structure later (costs money)

**These risks are ACCEPTABLE if:**
- You thoroughly research free resources (Phase 1)
- You get Section 8 and SSA clarification IN WRITING
- You commit to professional review once business has revenue
- You maintain meticulous expense documentation from Day 1

**These risks are UNACCEPTABLE if:**
- You skip research and "wing it"
- You make aggressive tax claims without documentation
- You delay professional review indefinitely
- You ignore warnings from SSA or Section 8 caseworker
