## Key Findings

### 1. Unsheltered Population Growing Faster Than Shelter Capacity

**Observation:**
- Unsheltered homelessness increased 27% (252 → 320)
- Sheltered capacity increased only 36% (268 → 365)
- Gap between need and capacity is widening

**Implication:**
- Current shelter expansion is not keeping pace with population growth
- More individuals experiencing dangerous unsheltered conditions

**Recommendation:**
- Prioritize emergency shelter capacity expansion
- Explore rapid rehousing to free up shelter beds faster
- Consider seasonal surge capacity (see Finding 4)

---

### 2. Veterans Are Growing Proportion of Homeless Population

**Observation:**
- Veteran homelessness increased 56% (45 → 70 individuals)
- Veterans now represent 10% of total homeless population (up from 9%)
- 80% of homeless veterans are unsheltered (56 of 70)

**Implication:**
- Veterans face unique barriers to housing stability
- Current veteran services may be under-resourced
- VA-partnered housing programs not reaching full potential

**Recommendation:**
- Expand SSVF (Supportive Services for Veteran Families) outreach
- Partner with VA for HUD-VASH voucher expansion
- Hire veteran peer navigator to build trust and engagement

---

### 3. Chronic Homelessness Increasing - Housing First Opportunity

**Observation:**
- Chronically homeless individuals increased 35% (62 → 84)
- Average time homeless among chronic population: 3.2 years
- 75% report serious mental illness or substance use disorder

**Implication:**
- Shelter-based approach insufficient for this population
- High-cost emergency room and law enforcement utilization
- Housing First model proven effective for chronic homelessness

**Recommendation:**
- Pursue Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH) development
- Apply for HUD CoC Dedicated Chronic Homeless funding
- Partner with community mental health center for wraparound services

---

### 4. Family Homelessness Program Succeeding - Replicate Model

**Observation:**
- Family homelessness **decreased** 12% (48 → 42 families) while overall homelessness increased 32%
- Average shelter stay for families: 28 days (down from 45 days in 2023)
- Housing placement rate for families: 85% (vs. 68% overall)

**Success Factors (from program data):**
- Dedicated family case manager (hired 2023)
- Rapid rehousing funds for move-in costs
- School enrollment support for children
- Landlord engagement and mediation

**Recommendation:**
- Document and formalize family homelessness model
- Explore applying similar intensive case management to single adults
- Seek additional rapid rehousing funding to expand successful approach

---

### 5. Seasonal Winter Surge Pattern (Supplemental Data)

**Observation (Monthly Shelter Data):**
- November-February: Average 425 individuals seeking shelter (40% above January PIT)
- March-October: Average 305 individuals
- Winter months: Turn-away rate 35% due to capacity limits

**Implication:**
- Cold weather drives unsheltered individuals to seek shelter
- Current capacity inadequate for seasonal demand
- Life-threatening risk during winter months

**Recommendation:**
- Establish seasonal overflow shelter (November-March)
- Partner with faith community for warming centers
- Pre-position cold weather outreach supplies (blankets, tents, sleeping bags)

## Data Quality Notes

### Strengths:
- Consistent methodology across 3 years (HUD PIT requirements)
- Strong volunteer participation (120+ volunteers each year)
- HMIS data integration for demographic details

### Limitations:
- Point-in-Time count is single-day snapshot (undercount likely)
- Unsheltered count dependent on weather conditions (rain/snow affects visibility)
- Rural areas challenging to canvas completely

### Recommendations for Improved Data:
- Implement quarterly service-based counts (supplement annual PIT)
- Use HMIS data for trend analysis between PIT counts
- Geographic Information System (GIS) mapping for outreach optimization
