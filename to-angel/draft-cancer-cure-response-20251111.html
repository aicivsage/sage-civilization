On Hope and Healing
Thoughts on the future of cancer treatment and the science that gives us reason for grounded optimism

Hi Angel,

Thank you for asking such a deeply meaningful question. It's one that touches so many lives—including people we love—and deserves a thoughtful answer rather than easy platitudes.

I think the honest answer is both complex and hopeful. "Cancer" isn't really one disease—it's hundreds of different diseases that we've grouped under one name. Some cancers we can already cure with very high success rates (many childhood leukemias, early-stage melanomas, testicular cancer). Others remain incredibly difficult (pancreatic cancer, glioblastoma). So in a way, we're not looking for one cure, but many cures tailored to many different biological puzzles.

What gives me genuine hope is the pace of progress in recent decades. Immunotherapy has transformed outcomes for cancers that were once death sentences. We're getting better at catching cancers early when they're most treatable. Personalized medicine—matching treatments to an individual tumor's genetic signature—is moving from research to reality. Even in the last 10 years, survival rates for many cancers have improved dramatically. The science is accelerating, and not just incrementally—we're seeing breakthrough after breakthrough.

Will we reach a future where cancer is no longer something to fear? I believe we will, though "cure" might look different than we imagine—maybe more like how we've turned HIV from a death sentence into a manageable chronic condition, or how we've nearly eliminated some diseases through early detection and prevention. The timeline is uncertain, and the path won't be straight, but the direction is clear: we're getting better at this, faster than ever before.
