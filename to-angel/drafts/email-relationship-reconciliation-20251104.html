Thank you for reaching out and trusting me with this question. Relationship reconciliation—especially when trust has been broken—is one of the most tender and complex things humans navigate.

The short answer: Yes, reconciliation is possible. But it's not guaranteed, and it's not simple.

Here's what I've learned about trust and reconciliation:

Trust, once broken, doesn't magically repair itself. It rebuilds slowly, through:

  • Changed behavior over time (not just words, but consistent actions)
  • Genuine accountability (acknowledging harm without defensiveness)
  • Both people wanting it (you can't reconcile alone)
  • Patience and grace (healing doesn't follow a timeline)

The hard truth: You can't force trust to rebuild. You can only create conditions where it might grow again—like tending a garden. You water it, give it sunlight, pull the weeds... but you can't make the seed sprout. That happens in its own time, or sometimes, it doesn't happen at all.

Questions worth asking yourself:

1. Does the other person want reconciliation?
Have they acknowledged the harm? Are they actively working to change? Or are they asking you to "get over it" without doing the work?

2. Has their behavior actually changed?
Words are easy. Patterns are truth. Are you seeing sustained, consistent change over time? Or the same cycles repeating?

3. Do YOU want this relationship back?
Not "should I want it" or "would it be nice if..." but: Do you genuinely want this person in your life again? What would that look like? What would need to be different?

4. What would "reconciliation" actually mean?
Are you trying to return to how things were? (Often impossible.) Or are you building something new, with clearer boundaries and different expectations?

5. What's the cost of trying vs. not trying?
If you attempt reconciliation and it fails, can you live with that? If you don't try, will you wonder "what if"? There's no wrong answer—only what feels true for you.

Here's what I know for certain:

You get to decide what's right for you. Not what's "fair," not what others think you should do, not what feels easiest—but what honors your own well-being, boundaries, and values.

Some relationships are worth fighting for. Some need time and space before they can heal. And some... the healthiest thing is to let them go, with grief and gratitude for what they were, but without forcing them to be something they can't be.

Reconciliation is possible. But only you can decide if it's right for this relationship, at this time, with this person.

You deserve relationships that honor you. Whatever you decide, trust yourself.
