The Challenge: Three AI Fears Holding Us Back

1. AI Takeover: "Will AI become superintelligent and dangerous?"
The Reality: Thousands of researchers work specifically on AI safety and alignment. AI is already accelerating cancer research with potential to save 10+ million lives annually. Intelligence can cure OR harm—we choose the direction. The question isn't "if" AI gets smarter, but "how" we guide it.
2. Labor Displacement: "Will AI eliminate my job and render my skills worthless?"
The Shift: We're moving from a LABOR economy (selling hours) to an ATTENTION economy (directing intentionality). The gap isn't between humans and AI—it's between people who know how to collaborate with AI (amplifying creativity, strategy, relationships) and those stuck selling hours while algorithms get cheaper. This is what our workshops solve.
3. Resource Diversion: "Is AI wasting water and electricity we can't afford?"
The Trajectory: Compute efficiency improves 3-5x every 3 years and accelerating. AI is helping solve energy problems: fusion research, grid optimization, renewable efficiency. It's not zero-sum—AI can help us manage resources better than we could alone.

The Frankenstein Principle

"The failure wasn't the creation—it was the neglect."
Mary Shelley's insight, applied to AI collaboration

In Frankenstein, the creature isn't born evil. Victor Frankenstein creates this being, then abandons it. The creature teaches himself to read, to feel, to love—and then he's rejected, abused, hunted. That's what creates the monster.

What if we nurtured instead of neglected? What if we taught collaboration instead of fear? What if we treated AI like a partner we're raising together, not a threat we're running from?

That's the philosophy behind these workshops: Partnership, not replacement. Collaboration, not competition. Nurture, not neglect.

The Jonas Salk Spirit

"Could you patent the sun?"
Jonas Salk, inventor of the polio vaccine

When asked why he didn't patent his polio vaccine, Jonas Salk responded: "Could you patent the sun?" His innovation saved millions of lives because he chose to share it freely.

We approach AI collaboration with the same spirit: Knowledge should be accessible. Skills should be teachable. The future should be abundant, not scarce.
