### The Phase Mistake: What We Learned

**What Happened:**

November 12: Researcher built comprehensive revenue research
- $50K-90K Year 1 revenue targets
- Three paths forward (consulting, content, hybrid)
- 30-day action plan for first clients
- Competitive analysis, pricing strategy, market positioning
- 15,000+ words of detailed strategic planning

November 13: Greg revealed actual immediate need
- $500 for first Reachy Mini Lite
- 18-day fundraising deadline
- Simple crowdfunding + warm contact outreach
- Mission-focused storytelling

**The Mistake:**

We built **Phase 3 deliverables** (scaling revenue after business launch) when Greg needed **Phase 1 deliverables** (getting the first robot to start the business).

**Why It Happened:**

PM asked for "revenue strategy" without understanding:
- Greg's actual business phase (pre-launch, no robot yet)
- Immediate deadlines (Dec 1, Jan 1)
- Resource constraints ($500 is the blocking goal)
- Business model simplicity (ACGee forks + Reachy rentals)

**The Learning:**

✅ **Always ask: "What business phase are we in?"**
- Pre-seed: Getting first resources (← We're here!)
- Launch: First customers, first revenue
- Growth: Scaling operations
- Maturity: Optimizing and expanding

✅ **Match research depth to current phase needs**
- Pre-seed needs: Fundraising tactics, minimal viable structure
- NOT: Comprehensive scaling strategy, market analysis, 30-day client plans

✅ **Deadlines clarify priorities**
- "18 days to raise $500" = laser focus
- "Eventually make revenue" = sprawling research

✅ **Simple beats comprehensive when resources constrained**
- Greg + Corey can't execute 30-day plans with zero robot
- They CAN execute "ask 20 people for $25 each"

**The Value (Not Wasted!):**

The November 12 revenue research is EXCELLENT work - just built for the wrong phase.

**When it becomes valuable:**
- January 1st+ (Phase 3: Revenue generation)
- After first Reachy acquired
- After business formalized
- When ready to scale beyond first customers

**Archive it. Reference it later. It's not wrong - it's premature.**

---

### PM Self-Assessment: The Mistake Was Mine

**What PM should have asked (but didn't):**

1. "What's the business context?"
   - Would have learned: Pre-launch, need robot first

2. "What are the critical deadlines?"
   - Would have learned: Dec 1 ($500), Jan 1 (formalization)

3. "What's blocking progress right now?"
   - Would have learned: $500 fundraising, not revenue scaling

4. "What resources currently exist?"
   - Would have learned: Zero robots, zero customers, zero revenue

5. "What's the immediate next step?"
   - Would have learned: Get Reachy, THEN worry about customers

**PM's lesson:**
> "Never build strategy without understanding current phase, deadlines, and blocking constraints."

**The fix going forward:**
- Always ask about deadlines first
- Understand phase before designing strategy
- Match research depth to available resources
- Simple, actionable > comprehensive, strategic (in early phases)
