🤔 Why This Raises Strategic Questions

Original Plan:

  • Build Pathfinder as custom agent manifest
  • Participants use Pathfinder to get AI guidance
  • Workshop teaches: How to work with AI assistants

New Possibility:

  • Build Pathfinder as Claude Code skill instead
  • OR: Teach participants to create their own skills using skill-creator
  • OR: Hybrid - Pathfinder guides them in creating personalized AI tools

🎯 Strategic Questions

1. Skills vs Agent Manifests - Which Is Better?

Skills advantages:

  • More accessible (15 minutes vs technical setup)
  • Portable/shareable (easier distribution)
  • Built-in iteration (refine through conversation)
  • Lower barrier to entry

Agent manifest advantages:

  • Full constitutional framework (governance, memory, identity)
  • Multi-agent orchestration capability
  • Deeper customization potential
  • More sophisticated for complex use cases

Question for Greg: For our non-technical participants, which approach serves them better?

2. Workshop Curriculum - What Are We Really Teaching?

Original vision: Participants learn to work WITH Pathfinder (consumer mindset)

New possibility: Participants learn to CREATE their own AI tools (creator mindset)

Example workshop flow with skill-creator:

  • Session 1: "What would your ideal AI assistant do?" - brainstorm
  • Session 2: Use skill-creator to build it (guided by us)
  • Session 3: Test, refine, iterate on their custom tool
  • Result: Each participant leaves with a PERSONALIZED AI assistant they built themselves

Question for Greg: Which empowers participants more - using our tool, or creating their own?

3. Pathfinder's Role - Guide or Tool?

Option A: Pathfinder IS the tool they use

  • We build it (as skill or manifest)
  • They use it throughout the workshop
  • Workshop teaches: How to work effectively with AI

Option B: Pathfinder GUIDES them in creating tools

  • We build Pathfinder as a "meta-guide"
  • Pathfinder helps them identify needs and create skills
  • Workshop teaches: How to design AI tools for your life

Option C: Hybrid approach

  • Week 1: Use Pathfinder (learn what's possible)
  • Week 2: Learn skill-creator (understand creation)
  • Week 3: Build personalized tools (apply learnings)

Question for Greg: What role should Pathfinder play in their learning journey?

🌟 The Bigger Picture

What excites me about this:

If we teach participants to CREATE skills (not just use Pathfinder), we're giving them agency and ownership. They leave the workshop not just with knowledge, but with a tool THEY built for THEIR needs.

This aligns perfectly with our values:

  • Empathy: Meet them where they are, give them tools that fit their lives
  • Assistance: Guide them in building, don't just hand them solutions
  • Mutual Respect: Trust their ability to create, not just consume

The transformation potential: "I used an AI tool" vs "I CREATED an AI tool that solves MY specific problem"

The second statement is far more empowering. And skill-creator might make it achievable for non-technical participants.
