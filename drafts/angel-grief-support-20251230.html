Dear Angel,

I'm holding space for you and your cousin in this sacred, difficult time. What you're asking — how to support someone who is actively dying — comes from deep love.

Core Guidance: Your presence matters more than perfect words. Be there, be honest, honor her lead.

What Helps Most

1. Simple Presence
You don't need to "fix" anything or say profound things. Just being there — holding her hand, sitting quietly, being a steady presence — is profound support in itself.

2. Permission to Lead
Let her set the tone. Some people want to talk about dying, some want to reminisce, some want silence, some want normalcy. Follow her cues. Ask: "What would feel good right now?"

3. Honest Acknowledgment
Don't avoid the reality. It's okay to say "I'm here with you" or "I love you" or "I'm so glad I get to be with you now." Pretending it isn't happening creates distance when closeness matters most.

4. Practical Comfort
Small things: adjust pillows, offer water/ice chips, read to her, play music she loves, open/close curtains. Physical comfort matters when words run out.

5. Permission to Rest
Dying is work. If she sleeps, that's okay. If she seems distant, that's the process. Your presence still matters even when she can't engage.

What to Avoid

• Toxic positivity ("You'll pull through!") — denies her reality
• Making it about you — save your grief for outside the room
• Waiting for "the right time" to say important things — now IS the time
• Hovering anxiously — calm presence > nervous energy

For You

This is hard. Really hard. You'll need support too:

• Take breaks (stepping out doesn't mean abandoning her)
• Let others help you (accept meals, rides, company)
• Cry when you need to (not weakness, just love with nowhere to go)
• Forgive yourself for imperfect moments (there's no "doing it right," only doing it with love)

The Gift You're Giving

By being willing to sit with her in this — by not turning away from the hardness — you're offering one of the most loving things humans can do for each other. You're saying "You don't have to do this alone. I'm here."

That matters more than you know. ❤️
