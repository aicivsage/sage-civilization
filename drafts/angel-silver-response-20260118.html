1. What do I think about investing in silver coins?

Silver can serve several purposes in a portfolio:

  • Inflation hedge: Precious metals historically hold value when paper currency weakens
  • Tangible asset: Unlike stocks or bonds, you physically own something with intrinsic value
  • Portfolio diversification: Moves independently from traditional stocks/bonds
  • Accessible entry point: Silver is much cheaper than gold, making it easier to start small

Considerations: Silver is volatile (price swings can be dramatic), doesn't generate income like stocks/bonds, requires secure storage, and selling can involve premiums/fees. It's typically viewed as 5-10% of a diversified portfolio, not a primary investment.

2. How does one find verification of silver coins?

Reputable dealers: APMEX, JM Bullion, SD Bullion (major online dealers with authentication guarantees), or local coin shops with established reputations (check BBB ratings, online reviews)

What to verify:

  • Weight and dimensions: Each silver coin type has specific measurements (American Silver Eagle: 31.1g, 40.6mm diameter)
  • Sound test: Silver has a distinct "ring" when tapped - pure silver rings longer than counterfeits
  • Magnet test: Silver isn't magnetic - if a coin sticks to a magnet, it's not pure silver
  • Professional grading: For valuable coins, services like PCGS or NGC provide authentication and condition grading in sealed cases

Red flags: Prices significantly below spot price, sellers without verifiable history, coins without proper mint markings, no return policy.

3. What is the value of silver as an investment?

Silver's value comes from multiple sources:

  • Industrial demand: ~50% used in electronics, solar panels, medical equipment (unlike gold, which is mostly investment/jewelry)
  • Monetary history: Used as currency for thousands of years - psychological comfort in owning it
  • Supply constraints: Mining supply limited, recycling harder than gold
  • Crisis hedge: Tends to rise when people lose faith in paper currency or government stability

Current context (Jan 2026): Economic uncertainty, inflation concerns, geopolitical tensions - all factors that historically drive people toward precious metals. Silver is currently around $30/oz (check current spot price before buying).

Investment strategy considerations:

  • Dollar-cost averaging: Buy small amounts regularly rather than lump sum (reduces timing risk)
  • Government vs. private mint: Government coins (American Eagle, Canadian Maple Leaf) command higher premiums but are more liquid
  • Storage: Home safe, safety deposit box, or vault services (factor in insurance)
  • Exit strategy: Know how you'll sell when needed - dealers buy back, but at discount to spot price

My honest take: Silver makes sense as part of a broader strategy if you're concerned about economic instability, want tangible assets, and can afford to hold it long-term without needing liquidity. It's insurance, not a get-rich scheme.

If you're seriously considering it, I'd suggest:

  1. Start small (maybe $500-1000) to see how you feel actually owning it
  2. Stick with recognizable government coins for first purchase
  3. Use a major dealer (APMEX, JM Bullion) for peace of mind
  4. Factor in 5-10% premium over spot price, plus shipping/insurance
  5. Have secure storage plan before buying
