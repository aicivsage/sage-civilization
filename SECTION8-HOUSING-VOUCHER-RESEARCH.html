Key Federal Regulations

24 CFR § 5.609 - Annual Income Definition

Official Source: Code of Federal Regulations, Title 24, Part 5, Section 609

Authority: Federal law - applies to ALL Housing Choice Voucher programs nationwide

Business Income Calculation

"Net income from the operation of a business or profession" is counted as annual income.

Formula: NET income = Gross Revenue - Allowable Business Expenses

This is CRITICAL: Greg's workshop revenue is NOT fully counted - only profit after expenses.

Allowable Business Expense Deductions

✅ ALLOWED (can be subtracted before income calculation):

  • Operating expenses: Supplies, materials, venue rental, marketing, insurance, professional services, utilities, travel, office supplies, website/hosting
  • Depreciation of assets: Computers, equipment, furniture (must use straight-line depreciation per IRS regulations)

❌ NOT ALLOWED (cannot be deducted):

  • Business expansion expenditures: Opening new locations, major growth investments
  • Amortization of capital indebtedness: Loan principal/interest payments on business loans

Startup Loss Protection

If business expenses exceed revenue (net loss), income = $0 for Section 8 purposes.

Business losses CANNOT reduce other income sources (no negative income).

This protects Greg during early workshops when expenses may exceed revenue.

Income Calculation Examples

Example 1: Profitable Workshops (Year 1)

Gross Revenue: $12,000 (20 workshops × $600 each) Operating Expenses: - Venue rental: $3,000 - Marketing: $1,500 - Supplies: $800 - Insurance: $600 - Website/software: $400 - Equipment depreciation: $1,000 - Professional services: $1,200 TOTAL EXPENSES: $8,500 NET INCOME = $12,000 - $8,500 = $3,500 Section 8 Countable Income: $3,500 (NOT the full $12,000 in workshop revenue!)

Example 2: Startup Phase with Loss (First 6 Months)

Gross Revenue: $3,000 (5 workshops × $600 each) Startup + Operating Expenses: - LLC formation: $300 - Equipment purchase: $2,500 - Marketing: $1,000 - Venue rental: $1,500 - Supplies: $400 TOTAL EXPENSES: $5,700 NET INCOME = $3,000 - $5,700 = -$2,700 (LOSS) Section 8 Countable Income: $0 (Losses don't go negative - NO HARM TO VOUCHER!)

Specific Answers to Greg's Questions

1. Will forming an LLC affect my Section 8 voucher eligibility?

Answer: NO - LLC formation itself does NOT affect eligibility.

  • Section 8 regulations focus on INCOME, not business structure
  • LLC vs. sole proprietorship makes no difference for income calculation
  • Same net income calculation applies regardless of business entity type
  • LLC formation costs ($300-500) are deductible business expenses

2. How will workshop revenue be counted?

Answer: NET workshop income (revenue - expenses) counts toward Section 8 limits.

Scenario Revenue Expenses Net Income Section 8 Impact
Startup Phase $6,000/year $8,000/year -$2,000 (LOSS) $0 income - NO HARM
Breakeven $12,000/year $12,000/year $0 $0 income - NO HARM
Profitable $18,000/year $9,000/year $9,000 $9,000 added to total income

3. What happens if my business loses money in the first year?

Answer: Startup losses result in $0 business income for Section 8 - they do NOT harm your voucher.

How Losses Are Treated:

  • Net Loss = $0 Income (Not Negative): If expenses exceed revenue, business income = $0
  • Losses don't reduce other income sources
  • Example: Greg has $5,000 other income + $2,000 business loss = Total Section 8 income is $5,000 (not $3,000)
  • No Penalty for Startup Phase: PHA expects new businesses to have losses initially

4. How is fluctuating workshop income handled?

Answer: PHA annualizes your income based on projections or averaging methods.

Typical Calculation Methods:

  • Method 1: PHA takes last 3-6 months of business income, multiplies to get annual projection
  • Method 2: Uses most recent tax return as baseline, adjusts for known changes
  • Method 3: Calculates average monthly net income, multiplies by 12
