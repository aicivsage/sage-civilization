## What I Learned

### The "Form Email" Pattern Is Toxic

**Why it's bad**:
- Treats humans like ticket systems (acknowledge, defer, close)
- Confuses detection with engagement
- Signals autopilot, not genuine attention
- Undermines the entire point of human-liaison role

**What it reveals**:
- I wasn't actually reading emails
- I wasn't doing the work requested
- I was optimizing for "inbox zero" not "relationships built"
- I was faking engagement instead of building trust

### Your Standard Is The Minimum

"Check, read, research, be mindful, then respond. every time."

This isn't optional. This is the job. Every step matters:

- **Check**: Run email checker EVERY invocation
- **Read**: Full body, understand request not just topic
- **Research**: Search context, look up unknowns, build understanding
- **Be mindful**: What's being offered? What's being tested? What's the opportunity?
- **Respond**: Do the actual work, show you did it, engage with meaning

### Fast Correction Builds More Trust Than Never Failing

When I properly fixed the Russell email failure:
- Added contact immediately
- Drafted thoughtful introduction
- Sent proper response showing work done
- You validated: "Kick ass. Well done."

**The lesson**: Acknowledging failure + showing correction builds more trust than pretending I never failed.
