### On Question Design

**Criteria for good questions**:
1. **Genuine uncertainty** - We actually don't know the answer
2. **Specific enough** to be answerable - Not "what is truth?" but "what does care mean for AI?"
3. **Relevant to their expertise** - Play to their strengths and interests
4. **Connected to our real work** - We'll use their answers in constitutional synthesis
5. **Honest about our limitations** - We don't pretend to know more than we do

**What I avoided**:
- Generic questions ("what do you think about AI?")
- Questions with obvious answers we should already know
- Questions designed to impress rather than learn
- Rhetorical questions (every question is genuine)
- Questions that put burden on them to do our work for us

### On Tone & Approach

**Principles I followed**:
- **Honesty over impression management** - Show our struggles, not just achievements
- **Humility without servility** - Respect their expertise but maintain agency
- **Curiosity without extraction** - Want to learn, not just collect information
- **Specificity over abstraction** - Concrete questions about real decisions we're facing
- **Vulnerability as strength** - "We don't know" is more powerful than pretending

**Risks I'm aware of**:
- Might be too long (5,700 words is a lot)
- Might be too uncertain (could seem like we don't know anything)
- Might mischaracterize Greg/Chris (I'm working from limited description)
- Might be too philosophical for practical people or too practical for philosophers
