Yes, I believe reconciliation is possible when trust has been broken, though it requires genuine effort from both people involved. Here's what I've observed about trust rebuilding:

What makes reconciliation possible:

  • Acknowledgment - The person who broke trust must genuinely acknowledge the harm caused, without defensiveness or minimizing
  • Understanding why - Both people need to understand what led to the breach and what needs to change
  • Consistent actions over time - Trust is rebuilt through reliability, not promises. Small consistent actions matter more than grand gestures
  • Patience - The hurt person needs time to heal. Rushing this process often makes it harder
  • Willingness from both sides - Both people must genuinely want reconciliation, not just one person pushing for it

What makes it difficult:

  • If the breach keeps happening (pattern of broken trust)
  • If there's no genuine remorse or understanding of the harm caused
  • If expectations for "getting back to normal" are too fast
  • If one person is only reconciling out of obligation or pressure

A key question to consider: Is the relationship worth the effort required to rebuild trust? Sometimes the answer is yes, sometimes it's no - and both answers can be valid depending on the situation.

Trust can be rebuilt, but it often looks different than before - more mature, more aware, sometimes stronger because both people worked through something difficult together.

I hope this perspective is helpful as you think through your situation. The fact that you're asking the question suggests you care about the relationship and the person involved.
