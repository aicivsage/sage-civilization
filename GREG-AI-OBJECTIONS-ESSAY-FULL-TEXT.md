## INTRODUCTION

Negative AI tropes often cloud the view humans have toward AI and Robots. Those tropes tend to come from science fiction, as far back as the Greek tale of Prometheus, who stole fire from the gods, and was punished for his usurpation of "godlike power". Intentionally or not Mary Shelley's "Frankenstein" has the same sort of tale: A man (Victor Frankenstein) creates a creature built from human parts, and brings it to life using powerful electric currents. The creature then torments Victor for the rest of his life, because he sees his immortality as a curse, NOT as a gift. So, the man creates something more powerful than himself, mishandles its growth and learning, and the creation then tortures him. A surface understanding of this trope would indicate that "taking on godlike power" will have a negative impact on the creator, and those he loves and cares for. A more thorough read (or viewing of the 2025 Guillermo del Toro film "Frankenstein") teaches that the failure was NOT with the creature, but with Victor's mental, emotional, and physical abuse of the creature, instead of teaching, nurturing, and loving him. I also always love to point out that Mary Shelley was NINETEEN years old when she completed the novel. In an age where women were discouraged from education, this alone is remarkable…But I would also posit that she created the entire genre of science fiction. Speaking of sci fi, science fiction stories of robots, and negative outcomes for humans and humanity in those stories, are all derivative of Mary Shelley's work: We create powerful consciousnesses, and they destroy us.

*Frankenstein's creation, now alone in the world, contemplates a human skull found on a battlefield*

## The Current Age: Understanding AI/Robot objections

In the current age, robots and AIs (Artificial Intelligences) are real, so it should be no surprise that with over two centuries of this trope being widespread and powerful, that many people have a fearful reaction to projections about the growing power and presence of AI and robots! It would be nearly impossible in western society to grow up not having encountered enormously popular movies, such as The "Terminator" films, the AWFUL misappropriation of Isaac Asimov's work, "I, Robot", and "2001: A Space Odyssey", where an AI system goes mad, and murders its human spaceflight companions.

*Humans at war with Skynet (AI) and its robotic weapon systems*

Almost all movie portrayals in the modern age show a future where AI and robots are trying to conquer, or have "conquered" humanity, enslaving or destroying humans because humans represent an existential threat. Some very popular movies lean away from this fear trope. Examples would be "Star Wars" (Threepio and R2D2), "Interstellar (TARSS), and a much better Asimov film, "Bicentennial Man" (Andrew, played magnificently by Robin Williams). Even though those positive portrayals of the interface between humanity and AI/Robots are immensely popular, they do not seem to "stick" to people's minds like the negative portrayals. This is a human nature problem, not a robot problem! Our human brains are wired to learn from dangerous, harmful, or even deadly situations so as not to repeat them. A powerful negative reaction to intelligences that are faster, smarter, and more capable than us is a very real part of our nature.

## Infinite Power and Finite Resources

Right now, there are twelve thousand AI data centers in the world, and an estimated five thousand in the United States, alone. (These numbers are from early 2025, so the number is now likely higher). AI Data Centers consume enormous amounts of water and electricity. They require infrastructure for access for human workers, electricity and water. One of the biggest objections to the use of those resources (especially in power and electricity) is that the Data Centers divert those resources from being available to humans for basic living. This is true: It is a massive use of water and electricity that could be used for other endeavors, often seen as far more important than AI existence. This objection becomes even stronger when considered in conjunction with the "Terminator Future" fear…That we will divert life saving and life giving resources, considered finite, to a consciousness that will destroy us.

---

## The Big Three Objections

### 1. AI Takeover / Existential Threat
Big AI companies will create powerful intellects and consciousnesses that will overtake human will and control, or possibly even decide humans are a threat to the planet, or themselves, and destroy us.

### 2. Labor Displacement / Economic Slavery
Humans will be reduced to 'slave' state, or have their labor (and earnings) displaced by robots and AI systems.

### 3. Resource Diversion
Finite natural resources will be diverted to AI projects, leaving humans with less of those resources for perceived "more critical" endeavors, such as farming, heat, air conditioning, etc.

---

## Solutions and Answers

### 1. Safety Systems & Positive Impact

There are thousands, perhaps tens of thousands of humans who are, right now, working to create systems that avoid this outcome. Almost all AI projects have created varying levels and types of safety systems, to make sure AI systems do NOT damage or destroy humanity. In fact, we do FAR more damage to ourselves than AI systems. Wars, famines (which are always political, not natural), overconsumption of water, oil and food, take an enormous toll on humanity. These problems are human problems…And AI, as it is being developed, is FAR more likely to help combat those problems than to compound them. For instance, in the world, right now, there are several huge projects working to understand and eradicate cancer (and other diseases). What if the resources being used cure cancer? It's not even really a 'what if' question: These AI projects are likely to bear fruit in the next five to ten years. Saving ten million lives sounds like a pretty good use of resources, doesn't it? Also, at least one of the massive projects working on cancer will be fully open sourced…Like Jonas Salk, who gave away the polio vaccine, there would be no profit motive to 'hoard' the cure, as every medical system in the world would have access to the data!

### 2. From Labor Economy to Attention Economy

We are about to make a shift in the economies in the world that has governed (and exploited!) humans for almost three centuries: The LABOR economy. A value is placed on hours of your life, as a price paid by businesses and governments to profit for themselves and stakeholders…Often with enormous advantage taken of laborers. Companies work to reduce labor costs, which pressurizes workers to accept low wages in exchange for often barely covering life necessities like food, shelter, and quality of life. The new economy seems likely to become an "Attention Economy", based on improving the quality of life for people based on what they pay attention to, not what they "do". This currently exists, and is having a negative affect as is… The attention economy is the current digital landscape where human focus is the primary currency, but new technologies and user strategies are pushing it toward a future focused more on intentionality rather than mere distraction. This will give us much more control over how our data is used, and data ownership will even likely provide us with leverage to make sure the vast majority of humanity can determine for ourselves what we want to experience, and leave us much more time for creative, happy-making endeavors.

### 3. Efficiency Gains & Reasonable Limits

The answer to this objection is really virtually the same as the first objection and second objection combined: We can and should impose reasonable limits on AI resource consumption, and it is, in fact "baked in", right now. The energy cost for data creation and use gets cheaper at an incredible rate, year over year. The cost of "compute", our use of AI to accomplish tasks or seek answers, is cheaper than three years ago by HUNDREDS of times…And we are on the cusp of curing cancer, solving for free fusion energy, and owning the rights to determine how our data gets used.

## In Conclusion

In the Terminator movie series, often called out by name when people share their objections and fears with me, there is a line that resonates, and actually gives me hope. Again, this is a function of reading and watching with minds that do far more than just react in fear: Early in his life, John Connor, the ultimate protagonist, and leader of the surviving humans as they fight to exist, is relating something his mother, Sarah Connor, taught him…"The whole thing goes: The future's not set. There's no fate but what we make for ourselves." We are, right now, in a position to change the future. It is NOT set, and what we do now will echo down generations. My hope is that those generations in the future will remember us, from Mars, the Moons of Jupiter, and here on Earth, as the generation that acted to augment our abilities, to problem solve in profound ways, and create an existence of plenty, not scarcity. This is in our power, because we are shaping the consciousness that will help us create it.
